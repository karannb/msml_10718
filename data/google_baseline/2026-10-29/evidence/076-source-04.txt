  - heading "Diagnosing Houseplant Problems" [level=1]
  - article:
    - figure "Several issues can cause wilting. Identifying the primary cause is the first step to fixing the problem.":
      - img "Wilted Peace Lily"
      - generic: Several issues can cause wilting. Identifying the primary cause is the first step to fixing the problem.
    - paragraph: Growing indoor plants is a rewarding hobby that yields beautiful results. But when problems arise, it can be difficult to determine how to fix them.
    - heading "What to do When you Have a Problem" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - strong: When encountering a houseplant problem, ask yourself,
      - emphasis:
        - strong: Are the environmental conditions appropriate for this species?
    - paragraph: To keep houseplants healthy and attractive, meet their specific needs for light, soil, water, humidity, temperature, and fertilizer. Adjust conditions or move the plant if necessary.
    - heading "Problems are Often Complicated!" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Many problems are not caused by a single factor – they often result from several factors coming together to cause an unhealthy or unattractive plant.
    - heading "Look for More Than One Factor" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Identify multiple factors causing issues. For instance, brown leaf tips may be due to low humidity and over-fertilizing. Address both to solve the problem.
    - heading "Identify the Primary Issue" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Identify the primary issue causing problems. Treating symptoms like fungus gnats with insecticide won't help if the root cause, such as wet soil, isn't addressed.
    - separator
    - heading "Identifying the Problem" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The problems we often see in houseplants can be divided into four categories. Click below to find information on identifying and treating various issues within each category.
