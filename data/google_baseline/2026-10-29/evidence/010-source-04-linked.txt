    - paragraph: Watering is where most beginners struggle with Crotons. They like consistently moist soil - but not soggy. Too much water suffocates the roots and causes rot, while too little water makes the leaves droop dramatically or fall off.

    - paragraph: During summer, Crotons may need water every few days, while in winter watering slows down. Don’t be surprised if your Croton sulks and drops a few leaves when you change your watering habits - they are sensitive to inconsistency.

    - paragraph: Soil makes a huge difference in Croton plant care. Heavy, compacted soil stays wet too long, which suffocates the roots. On the other hand, soil that dries out too quickly leaves your plant constantly thirsty.

      - strong: Croton plant care

    - heading "Humidity and Temperature | Croton plant care" [level=2]:
      - text: Humidity and Temperature |
      - strong: Croton plant care
    - paragraph: If your home is very dry, Crotons will let you know quickly. Low humidity makes the edges of the leaves turn crispy brown. Since Crotons are native to humid tropical environments, aim for 60-70% humidity for best results.
    - list:
      - listitem: Use a humidifier in winter, when heating dries the air.
      - listitem: Place your Croton on a pebble tray with water to raise local humidity.
      - listitem: Group it with other plants - they create a mini humid environment together.
    - paragraph: Temperature is just as important. Crotons hate cold drafts and sudden changes. Keep them in a steady environment between 65-80°F (18-27°C). If temperatures drop below 60°F (15°C), expect dramatic leaf drop.

      - strong: Croton plant care

      - text: Fertilizing Crotons |
      - strong: Croton plant care
    - paragraph: All that colorful foliage requires a lot of energy. Crotons are heavy feeders compared to many houseplants. During the growing season (spring and summer), feed with a balanced liquid fertilizer every 2-4 weeks.
    - paragraph: If your Croton’s leaves look dull or growth seems slow, a lack of nutrients might be the reason. Just don’t overdo it - too much fertilizer can burn the roots and cause leaf damage. Always follow dilution guidelines. In fall and winter, cut back on feeding, since the plant naturally slows down.

    - paragraph: Crotons can grow tall and leggy over time. Don’t be afraid to prune them back. Cutting stems encourages the plant to branch out and become bushier. The best time to prune is spring, when the plant is actively growing.
    - paragraph:
      - text: Always use sharp, clean scissors, and wear gloves. Croton sap can irritate skin, so it’s better to be cautious. Remove yellow or damaged leaves as you go - this helps the plant redirect energy into new, healthy growth. Pruning is not just for looks; it’s an important part of
      - strong: Croton plant care

    - heading "Repotting Crotons" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Crotons like to be a little snug in their pots, but eventually they outgrow them. If you see roots circling the surface of the soil or poking through drainage holes, it’s time to repot.
    - paragraph: Choose a pot just one size bigger, and refresh the soil completely. Repotting every 2-3 years keeps the plant happy and encourages growth. After repotting, expect some leaf drop as the plant adjusts - this is normal.
    - paragraph:
      - text: Repotting is an essential
      - strong: Croton plant care
      - text: step that gives roots more space and prevents them from becoming pot-bound.

    - heading "Frequently Asked Questions" [level=2]
    - heading "Why is my Croton dropping leaves?" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Leaf drop is usually caused by a sudden change such as a new spot, a draft, or a shift in watering. Crotons dislike change. Keep yours in one warm, bright, draft-free place with steady watering, and the dropping should settle as it adjusts.
    - heading "Why is my Croton losing its colour?" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Faded or greener leaves almost always mean not enough light. Crotons need plenty of bright light, including some direct sun, to keep their bold colours. Move it somewhere brighter and the new growth should come back more vivid.
    - heading "How often should I water a Croton?" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Water when the top inch of soil feels dry, keeping the soil evenly moist but never waterlogged. This is often about once or twice a week in the growing season and less in winter. Crotons like consistent moisture and good drainage.
    - heading "Why does my Croton have brown, crispy leaf edges?" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Brown, crispy edges usually mean the air is too dry or the plant is underwatered. Raise the humidity with a tray, grouping, or humidifier, and keep watering consistent. Crotons are humidity lovers and show stress quickly when the air is dry.
    - heading "Is Croton toxic to pets?" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Yes. Croton sap is toxic if chewed or eaten and can irritate the skin and mouth. Keep it away from curious pets and children, and wash your hands after pruning, since the milky sap can cause irritation.
