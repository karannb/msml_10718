  - paragraph: "There are many reasons for a Philodendron’s leaves turning yellow. Knowing the cause will help resolve the situation quickly. Here are some of the most common reasons:"
  - list: Overwatering Insufficient light Lack of nutrients Natural aging process Excessive fertilizing Pests
  - heading "How Do You Fix Yellow Leaves on Philodendron?" [level=3]
  - paragraph: Check your plant’s soil, and if it’s wet, refrain from watering until the top inches are dry. If the plant is seriously affected, you may need to repot it in fresh, well-draining soil and remove roots affected by root rot.
  - paragraph:
    - text: If your
    - link "Philodendron plant isn’t getting enough light":
      - /url: https://www.heyrooted.com/blogs/plant-care/philodendron-light-requirements
    - text: ", move it closer to a window. Philodendron plants should be protected from direct sunlight, which can scorch their leaves."
  - paragraph: Be careful when fertilizing; use a balanced fertilizer to provide all the necessary nutrients, and always dilute the recommended dose to avoid overfertilizing. Repot every few years into fresh, nutrient-rich soil.
  - paragraph: If one or two leaves turn yellow only occasionally, it’s probably part of the plant’s natural aging process. Check your plant regularly for insects and treat with organic insecticide if necessary.

  - paragraph:
    - text: Philodendron leaves drooping can be caused by low temperatures, transplant shock, inconsistent watering, or
    - link "low light":

    - text: . If you notice your Philodendron leaves turning mushy, the reason is overwatering.
  - heading "How Do You Fix a Droopy Philodendron?" [level=4]
  - paragraph:
    - text: Check the soil to make sure it isn’t soggy (you can use a
    - link "moisture meter":
      - /url: https://www.heyrooted.com/products/moisture-meter-small

  - heading "Why Are Leaves Falling Off My Philodendron?" [level=3]
  - paragraph: A Philodendron dropping leaves is often a sign of watering issues. Check the soil and watch for other signs of overwatering or insufficient watering. Inappropriate lighting or shock from sudden heat or cold can also cause leaf drop.
  - heading "Will Philodendron Leaves Grow Back?" [level=4]
  - paragraph: Philodendron leaves won’t grow back on the exact spot, but new leaves will normally bud from the top of the stem. If moved to more favorable conditions, the Philodendron plant will recover and continue growing.
