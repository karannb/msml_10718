  - paragraph: If your plant is looking limp and floppy, it’s telling you it’s not feeling happy. Here are six likely causes and how to fix them.
  - img "Wilting fiddle leaf fig"
  - paragraph: Nobody likes to see their houseplant looking wilted and sad. There are several reasons why this might be happening, most of which are easy to fix.
  - paragraph: So if you’ve got a droopy indoor plant let’s work out what’s wrong with it and how to perk it back up.
  - heading "1. Overwatering" [level=2]
  - paragraph: If your plant’s leaves are looking wilted and yellow, the problem is likely overwatering. Overwatered leaves will have a very soft, floppy look, because they’re waterlogged.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Solution:"
    - text: First, check the soil. If it’s really wet, then overwatering is likely. Just leave your plant to dry out. In future, only water it when the top two inches of soil feel dry.
  - heading "2. Underwatering" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Yes, too little water can also be to blame. If underwatering is the problem then leaves will look drier and probably won’t be yellow.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Solution:"
    - text: "Again, feel the soil. If it’s bone dry, you should increase the frequency of watering, but still stick to the same trick above: only water with a"
    - link "watering can":
      - /url: /products/accessories-patch-watering-can/
    - text: when the top two inches of soil feel dry.

  - heading "3. Lack of light" [level=2]
  - paragraph: If your plant is not getting enough sun it will wilt because it can’t absorb enough light to feed itself.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Solution:"
    - text: Try moving the plant somewhere a little brighter. Be gentle, so just shift it a little closer to a window. Don’t go straight from deep shade to a sunny window. That will stress your plant out.
  - img "A drooping plant on a bedside table in a dark bedroom"
  - heading "4. Ageing growth" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Wilting isn’t always a sign your plant is unhappy, if you’re seeing a few wilted leaves near the bottom of the plant it probably just means they’ve completed their lifecycle.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Solution:"
    - text: There’s nothing to worry about here. You can snip off the odd wilted leaf or flower with clean
    - link "secateurs":
      - /url: /products/accessories-secateurs/
    - text: . This will encourage your plant to put its energy into new growth.
  - heading "5. It's outgrown its pot" [level=2]
  - paragraph: Has your plant been in its current pot for a few years? If so, the pot may no longer be big enough for its root system. A too-small pot makes it hard for your plant to absorb nutrients properly, which can cause wilting.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Solution:"
    - text: Re-potting is best done between March and September, when your plant is in a growing phase. Our
    - link "fabric nursery pouches":
      - /url: /products/accessories-nursery-pouch/
    - text: are better for your plant's root systems and our
    - link "specially-formulated composts":

    - text: will be just right for your plant. Check our
    - link "guide to repotting":

    - text: for full instructions.

  - heading "6. Temperature" [level=2]
  - paragraph: A plant that’s too hot may wilt from drying out too quickly. A plant that’s too cold may wilt from stress.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Solution:"
    - text: Keep your plants somewhere with a warm, even temperature. This isn’t as complex as it sounds. Basically, if a room is a comfortable temperature for you it will be comfortable for your plant.
