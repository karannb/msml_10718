  - text: the insects away (don’t just smear them around).

- heading "Pesticide for Mealybugs" [level=3]
- paragraph:
  - strong: For most houseplants, the best “pesticide for mealybugs” is a contact approach – alcohol for spot killing plus insecticidal soap or neem sprays repeated weekly – because mealybugs hide and new crawlers hatch over time.
- heading "Preventing Mealybugs – How to Stop Repeat Infestations" [level=2]
- paragraph: The goal isn’t just to treat mealybugs, it’s to keep them from coming back. Prevention is especially important for anyone with multiple houseplants, where pests can quietly hop from pot to pot.
- heading "Prevention Checklist That Actually Works" [level=3]

  - strong: Clean leaves
  - text: – honeydew and dust make pests easier to miss.
  - strong: Avoid over-fertilizing
  - text: – soft, lush growth attracts sap-feeders.
  - strong: Control ants

- heading "Get Rid of Bugs on Indoor Plants" [level=3]
- paragraph:
  - strong: To get rid of bugs on indoor plants, you have to match the method to the pest – mealybugs require isolation, physical removal of cottony clusters, and repeated contact sprays because a single treatment rarely reaches eggs and hidden crawlers.
- heading "Mealybugs FAQ" [level=2]
- strong: What are mealybugs?
- paragraph: Mealybugs are small, soft-bodied insects that feed on plant sap and appear as white, cottony clusters on stems, leaves, and roots.
- strong: How do I know if my plant has mealybugs?
- paragraph: Common signs include white fluffy residue, sticky honeydew on leaves, yellowing foliage, stunted growth, and ants crawling on the plant.
- strong: Are mealybugs harmful to plants?
- paragraph: Yes. Mealybugs weaken plants by sucking sap and can eventually cause leaf drop, poor growth, and plant death if left untreated.
- strong: Where do mealybugs come from?
- paragraph: Mealybugs usually arrive on new plants, contaminated soil, pots, or gardening tools and spread easily between nearby plants.
- strong: Do mealybugs spread quickly?
- paragraph: Yes. Mealybugs spread rapidly, especially indoors, as they move between plants and reproduce quickly in warm conditions.
- strong: Can mealybugs live in soil?
- paragraph: Yes. Some mealybugs live on roots, making infestations harder to detect until the plant shows stress symptoms.
- strong: What plants are most affected by mealybugs?
- paragraph: Mealybugs commonly infest houseplants like succulents, orchids, ficus, hibiscus, citrus, and many tropical plants.
- strong: How do I get rid of mealybugs naturally?
- paragraph: Mealybugs can be removed by dabbing them with rubbing alcohol, washing plants with soapy water, or using neem oil regularly.
- strong: Does rubbing alcohol kill mealybugs?
- paragraph: Yes. Rubbing alcohol dissolves the protective coating of mealybugs and kills them on contact.
- strong: Can dish soap kill mealybugs?
- paragraph: Insecticidal soap kills mealybugs on contact when it wets the insect; DIY dish-soap mixes can sometimes damage foliage, so spot-test..
- strong: How often should I treat for mealybugs?
- paragraph: Treatments should be repeated every 7–10 days until no signs of mealybugs remain, as eggs may hatch after initial treatment.
- strong: Are mealybugs contagious to other plants?
- paragraph: Yes. Mealybugs easily move to nearby plants, so infected plants should be isolated immediately.
- strong: Can mealybugs infest outdoor plants?
- paragraph: Yes. Mealybugs infest both indoor and outdoor plants, especially in warm climates.
- strong: Will mealybugs go away on their own?
- paragraph: No. Mealybugs rarely disappear without intervention and usually worsen over time.
- strong: Are mealybugs dangerous to humans or pets?
- paragraph: No. Mealybugs are not harmful to humans or pets, but they are destructive to plants.
- strong: What is the fastest way to kill mealybugs?
- paragraph: Directly applying rubbing alcohol to visible mealybugs is the fastest and most effective immediate solution.
- strong: Can I save a plant with a severe mealybug infestation?
- paragraph: Yes, if treated early and consistently, many plants recover fully from mealybug infestations.
- strong: How can I prevent mealybugs from coming back?
- paragraph: Prevent mealybugs by inspecting new plants, cleaning leaves regularly, avoiding over-fertilizing, and maintaining good airflow.
- heading "Summary" [level=3]
- paragraph:
  - strong: "The key to mealybugs control is consistency: isolate the plant, physically remove cottony clusters, and repeat contact sprays every 7 – 10 days long enough to outlast eggs and crawlers."
