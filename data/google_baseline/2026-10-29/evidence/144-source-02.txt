    - heading "What's Wrong with My Plant? Questions and Answers" [level=2]
    - heading "1. Why Is My Houseplant Stretching?" [level=3]
    - 'figure "Credit: Jason Donnelly"':
      - img "Schefflera plant reaching for light by door"
      - text: "Credit:"
      - paragraph: Jason Donnelly
    - 'heading "Answer: Light is Needed." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: If your houseplant problem is that it's started
      - link "getting leggy and awkwardly tall":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/houseplants/care/fix-leggy-houseplants/
      - text: or is sending out spindly, weak stems reaching toward a light source, it's trying to tell you that it
      - link "needs more light":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/best-grow-lights-7255086
      - text: . Move it closer to a window or switch it to another window that gets more light. South-facing windows are the brightest, north-facing windows offer the least light, and east and west windows fall somewhere in between. Rotate your plants so that all sides of the plant get equal access to the sun and prevent the houseplant problem of lopsided growth.
    - paragraph:
      - text: If you still can't find the perfect spot for the plant,
      - link "invest in a grow light":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/houseplants/care/choosing-plant-grow-lights/
      - text: to make sure it gets what it needs.
    - heading "2. Why does my houseplant have curling leaves?" [level=3]
    - 'heading "Answer: Insects or disease." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: Unusual growth patterns are a
      - link "common sign of disease or insect presence":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/houseplants/care/common-houseplant-pests/
      - text: . You may see curling or distorted leaves and wonder, "What's wrong with my plant?" Look closer—you may find other signs of insects or the insects themselves. If you find black spots or fuzzy white spots, it's probably a disease. Use your fingers to crush pests or treat your plant with
      - link "neem oil.":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/pests/neem-oil-for-plants/
      - text: If your plant has a disease, remove affected areas to prevent further spread.
    - heading "3. Why does my houseplant have brown leaf tips?" [level=3]
    - 'heading "Answer: Water, salt, or humidity." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: If your
      - link "houseplant problem has brown tips on its leaves":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/houseplants/care/why-are-my-houseplants-leaves-turning-brown-on-the-tips/
      - text: ", it may be signaling one of the following issues:"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Inconsistent watering:"
      - text: This doesn't mean you must water your houseplants at 9 a.m. on a Tuesday. Instead, be sure to only water when a plant needs it. But even if your plant can tolerate less frequent watering, try not to go weeks without watering and randomly surprise it with a well-intentioned deluge. When you water, make sure water runs out of the drainage holes so you know it's not only reaching the top part of the plant.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Too much salt:"
      - text: This can happen from overfertilizing or salts from treated tap water. Flush the soil with water until it drains clear, or
      - link "repot your plant":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/houseplants/care/repotting-and-potting-up-a-plant/
      - text: into
      - link "fresh potting mix":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/best-soil-for-succulents-6890328
      - text: . Then, go easy on the fertilizer and try watering it with distilled water.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Low humidity:"
      - text: Many houseplants are tropical natives. If the air in your house or apartment is dry, place a humidifier nearby.
    - text: "Related:"
    - link "The Best Humidifier for Plants, Tested by BHG":
      - /url: https://www.bhg.com/homekeeping/house-cleaning/cleaning-products-tools/best-humidifiers-for-plants/
    - heading "4. Why are the leaves on my houseplant yellow?" [level=3]
    - 'heading "Answer: Possibly overwatering." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: If you notice just a couple of
      - link "yellow leaves":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/lucky-bamboo-turning-yellow-8553340
      - text: ", it’s usually not a big issue and can be pinched off. However, a lot of yellow leaves could indicate a significant problem, like overwatering, which is a common cause of houseplant woes. Most plants prefer soil that’s dry about 1/2 to 1 inch below the surface before watering. Avoid letting your plants sit in water to prevent root rot, so be sure the pots have drainage holes and that excess water is emptied from trays."
    - paragraph:
      - link "Yellowish leaves":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/geranium-leaves-turning-yellow-11744777
      - text: all over the plant may also indicate too little light. Try moving the plant to a brighter spot.

    - heading "5. Why is my houseplant dropping leaves?" [level=3]
    - 'heading "Answer: Low light or location." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: Are you constantly having to pick up leaves from the floor when you walk by your plant? Some plants,
      - link "such as ficus":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/plant-dictionary/houseplant/ficus/
      - text: ", are sensitive to a change in location and show it by dropping leaves. As long as the growing conditions in the new spot are still ideal, it should grow fresh leaves after it settles in. If you haven't moved your plant recently, leaf drop may also indicate low light."
    - text: "Related:"
    - link "32 Best Low-Light Indoor Plants to Brighten Up Your Home":
      - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/houseplants/projects/indoor-plants-for-low-light/
    - heading "6. Why are the leaves on my houseplant pale or burnt-looking?" [level=3]
    - 'figure "Credit: Dean Schoeppner"':
      - img "Chinese evergreen plant"
      - text: "Credit:"
      - paragraph: Dean Schoeppner
    - 'heading "Answer: Too much sun or fertilizer" [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: Like humans,
      - link "your plant can get sunburned":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/houseplants/care/houseplant-sunburn/
      - text: . Pale or white leaves are usually a result of too much light. Luckily, this is easily resolved by moving your plant out of direct sunlight or to a window that gets less intense light. Leaf scorch can also be a result of fertilizer burn.
      - link "Use less fertilizer":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/yard/garden-care/why-you-should-fertilize-plants/
      - text: or switch to distilled water, as the salt from softened faucet water can build up in the soil.
    - heading "7. Why are my houseplant's leaves wilting?" [level=3]
    - 'heading "Answer: It''s difficult to say." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: If your plant's leaves are wilting, there could be various causes that could be the culprit. Wilted leaves may be due to factors like inconsistent watering, poorly draining soil, heat stress, disease, or low humidity. A
      - link "plant moisture meter":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/best-plant-moisture-meters-7976267
      - text: can help diagnose some of these issues. To revive your plant, check the soil—if it’s too dry, water it thoroughly; if it's soggy, ensure better drainage. Try lowering the room temperature,
      - link "repotting with fresh soil":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/yard/soil/how-to-reuse-potting-soil/
      - text: ", or increasing humidity by misting."
    - heading "8. Why are there spots on my houseplant's leaves?" [level=3]
    - 'figure "Credit: Denny Schrock"':
      - img "close up of spots on leaves"
      - text: "Credit:"
      - paragraph: Denny Schrock
    - 'heading "Answer: Disease" [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: Spots on leaves are often
      - link "caused by plant disease":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/pests/insects-diseases-weeds/common-plant-diseases/
      - text: . You may be able to salvage the plant by improving air circulation so that it dries out more quickly between watering and removing diseased foliage. Throw away any diseased plant material so it doesn't spread to other plants. To prevent disease, give plants ample space to grow, remove dead foliage promptly, and always water at the base of a plant.

    - heading "9. How do I get rid of powdery mildew on my houseplant?" [level=3]
    - 'heading "Solution: Increase air flow." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - link "Powdery mildew":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/diy-powdery-mildew-spray-11745519
      - text: is a fungal infection that affects many plants. It's easiest for mildew to form in still, humid conditions, so increasing the airflow around the affected plant can also help. Remove the severely infected foliage when working on controlling the issue.
    - heading "10. Why isn't my plant absorbing water?" [level=3]
    - 'heading "Answer: Poor soil." [level=3]'
    - paragraph:
      - text: If you're confident you're giving a plant plenty of water and it's not root-bound (two common reasons for plants to dry out), your houseplant problem may be an issue with your soil. Many
      - link "potting soils use peat":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/how-to-garden/peat-moss/
      - text: ", which holds water well when moistened but is difficult to wet thoroughly the first time. Even if it's been well-moistened in the past, leaving the plant unwatered when you go on vacation or forgetting to water it regularly can dry out the soil to the point where it's difficult to rehydrate."
    - paragraph:
      - text: Small pots can be submerged in lukewarm water to re-moisten the peat
      - link "in the soil":
        - /url: https://www.bhg.com/gardening/yard/soil/how-to-reuse-potting-soil/
      - text: . It's more challenging to do that with large pots. Many nurseries sell surfactants (wetting agents) that are safe for the plant. Follow the instructions on the bottle to fix your soil.
