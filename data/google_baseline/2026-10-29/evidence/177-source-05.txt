        - text: English ivy (
        - emphasis: Hedera helix
        - text: )
      - text: is a plant that can grow many different ways. In your garden, it can act as ground cover thanks to its quickly spreading root system, or as a climber capable of scaling dozens of vertical feet. Ivy can also make a gorgeous houseplant, thanks to its trailing vines.
    - paragraph: With all its different decorative uses, you might want to spread the love and propagate your plant to create even more ivy decor. Thankfully, propagating ivy isn't difficult, and there are a couple ways you can approach it. Here's how to propagate ivy successfully.
    - heading "When to Propagate Ivy" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The best time to propagate ivy is in late summer, when the plant is growing climbing shoots. Do not wait to propagate in winter, and do not propagate ivy if the plant isn’t thriving.
    - heading "Before Getting Started" [level=2]
    - paragraph: The easiest way to propagate ivy is by taking cuttings. It’s important to make sure you clean the tool you’re using for the cutting—either pruning shears or a sharp knife. Having a sterile blade reduces the risk of bacteria harming your plant and makes your propagation more successful.

    - heading "Instructions" [level=2]
    - heading "How to Propagate Ivy in Water" [level=2]
    - paragraph: One way to propagate a new ivy plant is by placing a cutting in water.
    - list:
      - heading "Make Your Cut" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Using clean pruning shears or a sharp knife, cut a piece of your plant. For successful ivy propagation, your cut should be four to five inches long from a healthy vine. This can be done during your regular pruning routine.
      - heading "Remove the Leaves" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Fill a clear container with water. You can use a glass, a bowl, or a jar, but it must be clear so you can see your complete cutting when it’s placed in the water.
      - paragraph: Trim off any lower leaves from your cutting. You should not have any leaves submerged when you place the cutting into the water glass.
      - heading "Put the Cutting in Water" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Submerge your cutting into the water, and place the glass in indirect sunlight. Too much sun exposure can cause algae to grow in the glass.
      - paragraph: Replace the water every two or three days. This provides nutrients to your cutting and reduces bacteria buildup.
      - paragraph: In a few weeks, a new root system will begin growing from your ivy cutting.
      - heading "Plant the Cutting" [level=3]
      - paragraph: Monitor your cutting until the newly grown roots are a few inches long. Now, it’s time to plant them where you want your new ivy plant to sprout.
      - paragraph:
        - text: Use
        - link "well-draining potting soil":
          - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/best-potting-soil-6560582
        - text: for your garden or pot. Place the cutting up so the roots are deep into the soil and press the soil neatly around it.
      - paragraph: Make sure your ivy is in a partially shaded or full-shaded area, and keep the soil moist (but not wet!) through regular watering.
    - heading "How to Propagate Ivy in Soil" [level=2]
    - list:
      - strong: Make your cut.
      - text: Using
      - link "clean pruning shears":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/how-to-clean-garden-tools-4799140
      - text: or a sharp knife, cut a piece of your plant. For successful ivy propagation, your cut should be four to five inches long from a healthy vine. This can be done during your regular pruning routine. Trim off any lower leaves from your cutting.
      - strong: Place the cutting in sand.
      - text: Fill a planter with sand, place your cutting inside, and cover the planter with a plastic bag. The bag helps your cutting retain moisture. Keep the cutting well-watered and moist (but not wet).
      - strong: Plant the cutting.
      - text: Once your ivy begins to grow, remove it from the sand and replant it in well-drained soil. Make sure your ivy is in a partial- or full-shaded area, and keep the soil moist (but not wet!) through regular watering.
    - heading "Frequently Asked Questions" [level=2]
    - list:
      - generic: Can you put ivy cuttings straight into soil?
      - paragraph: Yes, you can put ivy cuttings straight into soil as long as you dip the cutting into a rooting hormone first. But you may have better results when propagating ivy in water or sand (or a sand-soil mixture).
      - generic: How do you propagate red ivy?
      - paragraph: "To propagate red ivy, follow the same methods detailed above for propagating English ivy: take a four to five inch-long cutting, foster the roots in water or sand, and then plant in well-drained soil."
