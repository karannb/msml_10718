  - text: "If you're a fan of bonsai, you know how important it is to develop a strong, healthy trunk and appearance of age and marurity. But did you know that how you grow your bonsai—whether in the ground or in a pot—can drastically affect the development of that trunk? In this video, we're going to dive into a fascinating experiment: planting one white pine bonsai in the ground, and keeping the other in a pot. Over the next few years, I'll be showing you how each method impacts the trunk's thickening, the development of the tree’s character, and the overall growth rate. So, let's get into it. For this experiment I needed two near identical trees. Say hello to “the twins” I picked up these young Japanese white pine trees from my nearest bonsai nursery, AllThingsBonsai., just off j31 in the M1 motorway. It’s a great little bonsai nursery and Adam and his team are always super friendly and helpful. Here we have two very similar young white pine bonsai. They’re at the same stage in their growth, about the same size, and have been cared for in very similar conditions up until now. But soon, they’ll take different paths. On my right, we have the tree that will be grown in the ground. On my left, the tree that will remain in its pot. When you grow a bonsai in the ground—what we call ‘in the soil’—the tree has access to much more space for its roots to expand. This means it can grow much more vigorously and thicken its trunk faster. The added space allows the tree to develop stronger root systems and encourages a faster buildup of energy, which ultimately leads to thicker trunks and more substantial growth.
