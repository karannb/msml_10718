  - text: How to transfer your water propagations into soil successfully.
  - text: 1. A large pot does not equal a large plant!
  - text: Use a pot that fits your plants roots comfortably. Not too small to have them all crowded. This will dry out the plant too fast. And using a pot too big, will cause the roots to stay wet for too long leading to root rot due to the lack of oxygen.
  - text: 2. Provide the plant with nutrients and a rooting hormone.
  - text: I recommend always fertilizing your plant after repotting it because most cuttings in water don’t have any nutrients. You can use a slow release fertilizer which doesn’t burn your plants or even a liquid fertilizer when you water it.
  - text: I used Rootzone to stimulate root growth and help with repotting shock. Rootzone is specifically designed to provide the plant with fulvic acid, amino acids, and vitamins (thiamine) to stimulate root growth, improve nutrient absorption and water retention.
  - text: 3. Don’t let the plant dry out!
  - text: Specially in the first month after transferring. Your plant was used to growing in water and if you let the soil dry out, the roots wont have access to any water and they will dry out as well. This means your plant will not get dehydrated and start getting wilted or yellow leaves. Making you think that this happened because you repotted it.
  - text: Ideally you should water it when 3/4 of the soil has dried out. This way you don’t keep your plant in too wet or muddy soil (which will cause root rot due to the lack of oxygen). I personally love clear pots for this reason, I can see when exactly 3/4 of the soil is dry.
  - text: If you’ve made it this far, I hope you found this information useful. Please help share this video with others and let me know if you have any questions. 💚
