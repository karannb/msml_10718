  - heading "Homemade fertilizers and fungicides" [level=3]
  - img "compost tea how-to"
  - paragraph: by John Dromgoole
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Helpful Plants
  - paragraph: "Weeds: weeds contain lots of potassium and other nutrients. Pull them before they set seed to include in the compost pile."
  - paragraph: "Comfrey: This medicinal herb adds potassium, nitrogen, and various nutrients when dug into the soil, used as a mulch, or used as a compost activator."
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Fertilizers/Soil Activators
  - paragraph: "Compost tea: You can make your own compost tea. Blend some of your compost with water in a 5-gallon bucket. Let it sit overnight. Strain it and add to a sprayer or mix with more water to make a soil drench."
  - paragraph: "Soft drinks: Soft drinks contain phosphates. That’s the nutrient that boosts flower production. It also helps stimulate roots, like potassium does. Then, it’s got all that sugar! The sugar feeds the soil microorganisms. Pour your dregs onto your plants or onto the compost pile. If you want to make a soil or compost drench, use 4 parts soda to 1 part water."
  - paragraph: "Blackstrap molasses: Use 2 tablespoons to a gallon of water as a soil drench to feed the soil microorganisms. You can also use a granular form. But the liquid blackstrap molasses is something you can pick up at any grocery store."
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Fungicides
  - paragraph:
    - text: "Apple cider vinegar: This one has multiple uses in the garden."
    - text: First, you can dilute it with water and use as a fungicide to control black spot.
    - text: It also helps slightly reduce the pH of our alkaline soil. Lowering the pH helps make iron more available to the plants. Use 2 tablespoons to a gallon of water. As a soil drench, apply on a monthly basis at most. Don’t overdo it!
  - paragraph: As a foliar spray to control black spot, remember not to spray leaves in the heat of day or when temperatures are over 85?.
  - paragraph:
    - text: "Corn meal: Use whole ground cornmeal. Add about 2 lbs. per 100 sq. ft. This helps kill soil diseases."
    - text: Make a tea with one cupful of cornmeal to a gallon of water. Let it sit overnight. Strain, and use as a fungal spray.

  - heading "October To Do List" [level=3]
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Plant: ornamental & wildlife"
  - list:
    - listitem: Perennials, shrubs, ornamental (clumping) grasses
    - listitem: "Cover crops for dormant vegetable beds: clover, hairy vetch, elbon (cereal) rye, Austrian winter peas, or annual rye"
    - listitem: "Late: native wildflower seeds like bluebonnets, phlox, Mexican hat, Indian paintbrush, Indian blanket (gaillardia). Also, non-native poppies, larkspurs, hollyhocks"
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Plant: herbs"
  - list:
    - listitem: Perennials thyme, oregano, lavender
    - listitem: Cilantro seeds or transplants, parsley, dill, fennel , chervil, summer savory, borage
  - paragraph:
    - strong: "Plant: food crops"
  - list:
    - text: Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Vegetable Planting Guides (Central Texas)

  - paragraph:
    - strong: Prune
  - list:
    - listitem: Oak trees safe to prune
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Divide/Move
  - list:
    - listitem: Iris, daylily, fern, liriope, spring-blooming perennials, violets
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Prep
  - list:
    - listitem: Add compost to vegetable gardens along with organic fertilizer if not already done
    - listitem: Decide wildflower seeds and bulbs to plant in November
    - listitem: Mulch tropical and semi-cold-hardy plants like gingers, Esperanza, Pride of Barbados, Firecracker fern, bananas.
    - listitem: Take cuttings of tender annuals to propagate in warmth to renew your garden next spring
    - listitem: If temps dip, cut basil and preserve in oil in the freezer
    - listitem: Collect seeds of annuals to dry and store inside until next spring.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Lawn
  - list:
    - listitem: Fertilize with an organic slow release formula like 8-2-4 or similar ratio. Avoid products with too much nitrogen. This is the best time of year to fertilize.
    - listitem: "Brown patch: Apply a quarter inch of compost. Apply corn meal. Find out what’s causing the problem like low drainage spots or compacted areas."
    - listitem: Mow high to fend off weed seeds that are germinating. Taller grass shades them out.
  - paragraph:
    - strong: Other tasks
  - list:
    - listitem: "Late month: get row cover or plan other protection in case of early frost in November"
    - listitem: Cut basil to freeze in oil in ice cube trays to use this winter in soups and stews
    - listitem: Collect seeds from summer annuals to dry and store indoors until next spring
    - listitem: Get houseplants ready to move inside. Investigate for any “buddies” that could be coming indoors with them.
