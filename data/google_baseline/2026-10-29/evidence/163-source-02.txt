    - heading "What Mold on Soil Looks Like" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Mold on soil usually appears as small to large white, fuzzy patches on the growing medium’s surface. It can also appear on the soil poking out the container’s drainage holes. Excessively moldy soil is almost always accompanied by overly wet conditions.
    - 'figure "Credit: Lena_Zajchikova / Getty Images"':
      - img "A dracaena houseplant with white fluffy mold on the soil."

    - heading "Why Houseplant Soil Gets Moldy" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Even the healthiest houseplant soils aren’t immune to a little mold growth. However, a lot of mold on the surface of your soil often suggests you might not be offering the right conditions for your plant to thrive, usually due to moisture issues.
    - paragraph: Here are some common things to look for if you notice moldy houseplant soil.
    - heading "Overwatering" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - link "Overwatering your plant":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/signs-of-overwatering-houseplants-8647868
      - text: can quickly encourage mold growth. When you consistently provide more moisture than it needs, the wet soil becomes the perfect breeding ground for mold spores to thrive.
    - paragraph: This issue happens more commonly when the temperatures are colder and the soil is slower to dry out.
    - heading "Poor Drainage" [level=3]
    - paragraph: If moisture does not drain out of the soil efficiently, and it stays consistently soggy, mold spores will thrive in this environment.
    - heading "Contaminated Soil" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - text: Mold growth can originate from a contaminated bag of
      - link "potting soil":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/difference-between-potting-soil-potting-mix-847812
      - text: or one exposed to moisture and then
      - link "not properly stored":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/how-to-store-potting-soil-11754358
      - text: .
    - 'figure "Credit: Dmitry Marchenko / EyeEm / Getty Images"':
      - img "A monstera plant in a gold metal pot being watered with a white and gold watering can."
      - text: "Credit:"
      - paragraph: Dmitry Marchenko / EyeEm / Getty Images
    - heading "High Humidity Levels" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - text: High humidity levels create the perfect environment for mold to grow. The ideal humidity level for houseplants is between 35% and 65%; anything higher increases the risk of mold growth.
      - generic: "[ 1 ]"
    - heading "Plant Matter" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Plant matter, such as leaves left in the pot, can cause mold issues. As these leaves rot, mold will begin to grow.
    - heading "Is Moldy Soil Bad for Houseplants?" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Moldy soil might look unsightly, but it isn’t normally bad for houseplants. It’s often a buildup of saprophytic fungi, which are natural organisms that feed on dead and decaying plant matter and can benefit your soil.
    - paragraph: However, excessive mold growth can compete with your plant for the soil’s nutrients over time, hindering growth.
    - paragraph:
      - text: The problem with excessive mold on houseplant soil is that it usually indicates something needs to be adjusted in your plant’s growing environment. This is most commonly overmoist conditions, which can result in more serious ailments like
      - link "root rot":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/treat-root-rot-houseplants-5223283
      - text: and attract humidity-loving pests, like
      - link "fungus gnats":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/control-gnats-in-plants-4174817
      - text: .
    - heading "How to Get Rid of Mold on Plant Soil" [level=2]
    - paragraph: "Here are a few easy methods to get rid of moldy soil:"
    - list:
      - strong: Scrape it away
      - text: ": If the mold on the surface isn’t significant, scrape it off, add a fresh layer of potting mix once the rest of the soil is dry, and ensure that going forward it doesn’t get too moist."
      - strong: Use a fungicide
      - text: ": Many gardeners swear by a light dusting of cinnamon on the soil as a natural fungicide."
      - generic: "[ 2 ]"
      - text: You can also try a
      - link "baking soda and water mixture":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/baking-soda-spray-on-houseplants-1902672
      - text: . If neither of those options works, try a commercial soil fungicide.
      - strong: Replace the soil
      - text: ": Sometimes, starting from scratch and"
      - link "repotting your plant":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/how-to-repot-a-plant-8629819
      - text: is the simplest option, especially if the soil is very soggy, has poor drainage, and is extensively covered in mold.
    - heading "Preventing Moldy Soil" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Follow these tips to prevent moldy soils and promote healthier houseplants.
    - list:
      - strong: Avoid overwatering
      - text: ": Always test the soil moisture levels by pushing your finger into the soil. Usually, it’s best to water when at least the top few inches of soil are dry."
      - strong: Use a well-draining potting mix
      - text: ": Aerating"
      - link "soil amendments":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/guide-to-soil-amendments-7095754
      - text: like perlite and sand can improve drainage, or you can start from scratch with a new well-draining
      - link "potting soil for indoor plants":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/potting-soil-indoor-plants-container-plants-1902802
      - text: .
      - strong: Pick the right pot
      - text: ": Plant containers should have drainage holes allowing excess water to escape."
      - strong: Improve air circulation
      - text: ": If your houseplant collection is overcrowded or your home doesn’t have adequate ventilation, damp conditions can encourage moldy soils. Space plants should be used appropriately, and fans or"
      - link "dehumidifiers":
        - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/best-dehumidifiers-for-basements-6954927
      - text: should be considered in stuffy homes.
      - strong: "Remove dead plant material:"
      - text: This prevents the damp, decomposing matter on the soil’s surface from encouraging mold growth.
      - strong: "Increase sunlight:"
      - text: Increasing your houseplant’s exposure to sunlight can help prevent mold because most types of mold do best in dark, damp environments.
    - text: Tip
    - paragraph: Use plastic nursery pots inside of stylish cover pots that don’t have drainage holes. When it’s time to water, remove the plant and its plastic pot and water it over a sink. Once the excess water stops draining from the drainage holes, you can return it to its original spot.
    - heading "Is Moldy Houseplant Soil Bad for People?" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: Though we usually think that all mold is harmful, mold on houseplant soil is not particularly harmful to humans. The fuzzy white mold on houseplant soil is usually a saprophytic fungus, which gets its food from dead organic matter.
      - generic: "[ 3 ]"
      - text: When you see the mold on houseplant soil, don’t panic. Just scrape the mold away.
    - heading "Frequently Asked Questions" [level=2]
    - list:
      - generic: Do I have to throw away a houseplant with mold on the soil?
      - paragraph: There’s no need to throw out your houseplant if you notice mold on the soil. Once you’ve removed the moldy patches or replaced the soil, avoid overwatering in the future.
      - generic: Should I use vinegar to get rid of moldy soil?
      - paragraph: Avoid using vinegar to remove mold from your plant’s soil. Acetic acid can kill your plant if too much is applied. Instead, use cinnamon, a natural fungicide, on the soil, or purchase a fungicide spray from your local nursery.
      - generic: Should I throw away moldy potting soil?
      - paragraph: You don’t need to throw away moldy potting soil, but it is an option. First, try removing the mold, letting the soil dry out slightly, and adding soil amendments to improve drainage. If nothing works, throw away the affected soil and use fresh soil for your plant.
      - generic: Will mold on houseplant soil go away on its own?
      - paragraph: It’s best to remove mold from houseplant soil, as it is unlikely to disappear without help. While the mold quantities might not multiply if you change the soil conditions, removing it helps the soil breathe better and fixes the unsightly appearance.
      - generic: Do I have white mold or a mealybug infestation?
      - paragraph:
        - text: Mealybugs look like white powder on your houseplant’s soil, much the same way white mold looks. Besides mealybugs, check for other
        - link "types of tiny white bugs":
          - /url: https://www.thespruce.com/tiny-white-bugs-in-houseplant-soil-7484162
        - text: that may be found in the soil or on the leaves, as well.
