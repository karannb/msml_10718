        - heading "What's the difference between traditional azaleas and reblooming azaleas?" [level=2]:
          - strong: What's the difference between traditional azaleas and reblooming azaleas?
        - paragraph: The main difference between traditional azaleas and reblooming azaleas is the bloom time.
        - paragraph: Traditional azaleas bloom in the spring and last for a few weeks. They set their buds on the previous year’s growth, which means they should be pruned after the spring bloom.
        - paragraph:
          - text: Reblooming azaleas bloom in spring and again in late summer/fall, offering months of blooms rather than just weeks. A light trim after the spring bloom will encourage new growth and a more abundant rebloom.

        - paragraph: Most reblooming azaleas have a hardiness range of 6b-9, though this will vary slightly by azalea.

        - heading "Sunlight Exposure" [level=3]:
          - strong: Sunlight Exposure
        - paragraph: Azaleas can grow in areas with full sun or part sun. For the best performance, a location with part sun or filtered light is preferred.
        - paragraph: In cooler climates, azaleas can take all-day sun, but in warmer climates, they are happiest in areas with afternoon shade.
        - list:
          - listitem: "Full sun: 6+ hours of bright sun each day"
          - listitem: "Part sun/part shade: 4-6 hours of bright sun or filtered light all day"
          - listitem: "Full shade: fewer than 4 hours of sun each day"

          - strong: Size
        - paragraph: Reblooming azaleas are typically on the more compact side, so they’re perfectly suited for gardens of all sizes. Stick it at the front of the border, create a welcoming front landscaping by using it as a foundation plant, or even light up a patio by growing it in a container.
        - paragraph: Use the azalea’s mature height and width when planning your garden to ensure your azalea doesn’t outgrow its space.

        - heading "Reblooming Azalea Planting & Care" [level=2]
        - heading "How to plant:" [level=3]:
          - strong: "How to plant:"
        - list:
          - listitem: Find your planting site.
          - listitem: Prep the site by removing existing plants, weeds, mulch, or leaves.
          - listitem: Dig a hole twice as wide as your azalea’s container and just as deep.
          - listitem: Measure the depth so the top of the root ball is even with the soil.
          - listitem: Remove the azalea from the container and loosen the roots.
          - listitem: Place the shrub in the hole and backfill.
          - listitem: Water your new azalea well.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: "Soil:"
          - text: Reblooming azaleas prefer acidic soils that stay evenly moist, but with good drainage.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: "Watering:"
          - text: Water your reblooming azalea well for its first season in the ground. After that, keep the soil on the moist side, but not soggy.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: "Mulching:"
          - text: As azaleas have shallow root systems, a 2-3” layer of mulch over the roots of your azalea will help keep the roots cool and moist.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: "Pruning:"
          - text: Pruning isn’t necessary for reblooming azaleas, but a light trim after the spring bloom will help encourage new growth and a better fall rebloom.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: "Fertilizing:"
          - text: Azaleas benefit from an application of fertilizer to the soil surface every growing season.
        - paragraph: A newly planted azalea doesn’t require fertilizing in its first season, as the soil mix contains plenty of fertilizer.
        - paragraph: Starting the next year, apply an all-purpose flowering shrub fertilizer in late winter/early spring when the soil becomes workable, another after the spring bloom, and one more in late May/early June.

        - heading "Reblooming Azalea FAQs" [level=3]:
          - strong: Reblooming Azalea FAQs
        - paragraph:
          - strong: When is the best time to plant reblooming azaleas?
        - paragraph: In mild climates, azaleas can be planted at almost any time; however, spring or fall are the recommended planting seasons. Ensure you are providing enough water to your newly planted azalea to give it the best start in your garden.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: Will reblooming azaleas grow in full sun?
        - paragraph: In cooler climates, reblooming azaleas can be grown in full sun. Although all-day dappled light or part sun with afternoon shade is best, especially in warmer climates.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: When is the best time to transplant azaleas?
        - paragraph: Early spring or fall is the best time to transplant an azalea. It’s best to get to this task early in the morning or late afternoon when it’s cooler. Ensure you give your azalea plenty of water to help it reestablish its roots in its new home.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: Why is my reblooming azalea not blooming?
        - paragraph: There could be several reasons why your reblooming azalea isn’t blooming; a common reason is insufficient sunlight.
        - list:
          - listitem: Azaleas in shady spots will have weaker growth and poor flowering.
          - listitem: If you experienced a harsh winter or a sudden freeze, your azalea may have suffered winter damage and lost its spring flower buds. Give it time, and it may recover.
          - listitem: If your azalea is getting enough sun and didn’t experience a harsh winter, send us a picture; we’d love to help you get to the bottom of it.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: Can azaleas survive a freeze?
        - paragraph: Azaleas are quite susceptible to cold damage, so sudden freezes can cause dieback. If your azalea experiences winter damage, prune out the affected branches and give it time to recover.
        - paragraph: If your area has harsh winters, plant your azalea in an area that is protected from the worst of the winter weather and ensure you give it a good layer of mulch.
        - paragraph:
          - strong: Can reblooming azaleas be grown in pots?
        - paragraph: Yes! Reblooming azaleas are great container plants in USDA zone 7 and warmer.
