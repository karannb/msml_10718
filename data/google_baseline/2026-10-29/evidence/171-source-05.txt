    - heading "What causes indoor plant leaves to droop, and how can I fix the problem?" [level=1]
    - heading "Indoor Plant Care | Care Techniques" [level=3]
    - text: Quick Answer
    - paragraph: Spotted your leafy friend looking a bit droopy? It's time to play houseplant doctor! Could be too much or too little water, the wrong type of sunshine, a chilly draught, a lack of moisture in the air, damaged roots, or even a cheeky pest attack. Figure out the culprit, and you can sort your plant right out – a good drink, a change of scene, a bit of TLC... and it'll be perking up in no time!
    - generic: Further Information
    - paragraph:
      - text: Droopy leaves are a total downer. One day your
      - link "Monstera":
        - /url: https://www.houseplant.co.uk/collections/monstera-plants
      - text: is a queen, the next it's looking all sad and floppy. But don't worry – it's usually fixable! Let's play plant detective and bring that leafy friend back to life.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: Why the Droop?
    - list:
      - text: "Watering Woes: Top of the culprit list!"
      - list:
        - listitem: "Overwatering: Soggy soil chokes the roots. Yellowing leaves as well as droop are usually a sign of this. Solution: Let it dry out, then be more careful next time."
        - listitem: "Underwatering: Crispy, drooping leaves are a big clue. Solution: Give it a good drink (but don't drown it, of course!)"
      - text: "Light Problems:"
      - list:
        - listitem: "Too Little: Plant gets weak and can't hold itself up. Solution: Gradually get it closer to a window, or use a grow light."
        - listitem: "Too Much: Scorched leaves sometimes droop too. Solution: A shadier spot is needed."
      - text: "Temperature Tantrums: Plants hate sudden changes"
      - list:
        - listitem: "Cold Draughts: Leaves often drop rather than just droop. Solution: Move it somewhere less breezy."
        - listitem: "Radiator Blast: Crispy AND droopy is the usual sign. Solution: Comfier spot needed, away from heat sources."
      - listitem: "Low Humidity: Tropical plants like Ferns often sulk if the air's too dry. Solution: Misting, pebble trays, or a humidifier will perk them up."
      - text: Root Issues
      - list:
        - listitem: "Repotting Shock: Damaged roots can cause droop. Solution: Be gentle when repotting, and give it time to recover."
        - listitem: "Rootbound: Droopiness + roots poking out the pot = time to repot! Solution: Bigger pot, fresh soil."
    - paragraph:
      - link "Those Pesky Pests":
        - /url: https://www.houseplant.co.uk/blogs/indoor-plant-care/what-are-the-most-common-pests-that-affect-indoor-plants
        - strong: Those Pesky Pests
    - paragraph: "Little bugs sucking the life out of your plant WILL make it droop. Check for:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Webbing (spider mites)
      - listitem: Fluffy white blobs (mealybugs)
      - listitem: "Weird bumps (scale insects) Solution: Spray them with water for starters, then try neem oil or insecticidal soap. Separate the sick plant from healthy ones!"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: Need Help? We've Got Your Back
    - list:
      - listitem: "Photo Diagnosis: Snap a pic of your sad plant (include the whole pot and soil, if possible) and email it to us for advice."
      - listitem: "Know Your Plant: \"Monstera drooping\" + a quick internet search gives loads of specific care guides"
      - listitem: "Plant Geek Hotline: The Houseplant.co.uk team is obsessed with helping you succeed!"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: Don't Give Up!
    - paragraph: "Sometimes it takes a bit of trial and error to find the exact cause of the droop, but your plant is worth it! Remember:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Early action is best! Keep a close eye on your plants
      - listitem: We're here to support you every step of the way
