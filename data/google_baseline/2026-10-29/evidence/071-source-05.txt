        - generic: It’s etiolated (stretching for light) and very dehydrated. Due to this it is very hard to ID as it does not look as it should. Misting does not allow the roots to get a solid drink, so it’s very thirsty. These also require 8+ hours of direct sun to be compact and not lose leaves at a tiny bump of the plant. It will need acclimation to more sun to avoid burning.
        - generic: Every climate and plant is different, and people’s preferences play a role. So you will get a ton of different answers.
        - generic: But the plant itself needs sun and water.
        - text: Does my window provide enough light? Typically it does not, as they get a lot less rays being inside. Direct sun in a window does not equate to direct sun outside. However a south facing window that get lots of direct sun rays can work, with good top rays too. Sun hitting them from the top/on the crown of the plant is key to health. If you have lots of trees around that also plays a big roll as they can block a lot of sun. It may be enough! But if you see them lose color, stretch (seeing the stem between the leaves), downturned leaves (they are creating more surface area to absorb more sun), or overwater easy, these would be signs to add a grow light for them
        - img "☀️"
        - img "☺️"
        - text: it’s important to note succulents can sunburn, especially if experiencing lack of light. If the leaf is not grown in direct sun, it can’t handle it. Think pale skin. Most are grown in greenhouses. So, you must slowly acclimate them or they will sunburn, when using direct sun. Hot sun burns fast, when temps are above 80F I have to be extra carefulBasically, they need to get a base tan going. Starting outside under a tree or shade cloth; Experiment with direct morning sun first, add more every few days. They want 7+ hours of sun. Hot sun burns fast, when temps are above 85F, in winter I have no trouble sticking them outside (long as they aren’t etiolated). Grow lights are unlikely to burn them. I use Barrina T8 labeled pinkish/white. Love them! It’s a mostly white light with a small bit of red and blue added. Barrina and Sansi are well recommended also. Grow lights usually need to be 12 inches or closer, (I like about 4-6inches) on 12-14 hours, and always off at night. They need darkness to complete photosynthesis. You want a full spectrum light, white, or pinkish white.
        - text: A dehydrated plant will have soft lower leaves, a very dehydrated plant will have soft middle leaves as well. The top rosette is last to show signs. Succulents drink up the lower leaves and move their way up. The taco test -
        - img "🌮"
        - text: Gently squeeze the sides of a leaf (lowest is best) and try to make a taco shape.
        - img "🌮"
        - text: If you can make a taco the leaf is not full of water and needs a deep drink A dehydrated leaf feels like a deflated balloon, and bendable. Leaves will wrinkle also, sometimes only on he underside of the leaf. Make sure to check the lowest leaves as these dehydrate first, but not a leaf that’s already being absorbed.
        - generic: Overwatered leaves also start at the base, usually. As these are the leaves the fill up the most for future droughts. The leaves will typically turn yellow/translucent. The main difference is the leaves will not pass the taco test. As they will be plump and full of water. Leading to the conclusion that the plant had too much water and is bursting. An overwatered leaf feels squishy, if very bad, like jello.
        - generic: A healthy and hydrated leaf is firm, like solid firm.
        - generic: Overwatering isn’t really how much or how often you water, it’s when your soil retains water for too long/doesn’t stay airy when wet. Lack of oxygen in the soil is what causes rot, and succulents are extra sensitive to this and like very airy soil. So that’s why it’s always recommended to add grit to your soil at least a 50/50 ratio. Lack of light/energy aids in this, as they are not ‘using’ the water since they don’t have enough energy from the sun.
        - generic: Succulents naturally absorb/suck up their lower, older leaves for nutrients. To help them create new growth and blooms/babies. Best to wait till they are shriveled up like paper, then pull off, or they will start to drink another leaf instead. You do want to remove old dead leaves, they make a perfect home for pests and restrict airflow. Usually a little wiggle and tug and they come right off. They especially take leaves when making babies/blooms. If they start taking a lot (more than 2 at a time), I would shorten its dry period just a tad, that should help. While it is a natural occurrence, it is a clear sign of thirst as well. If I’m unsure to water, I wait for a lower leaf to start being absorbed, and that’s my sign it’s definitely ready for water
