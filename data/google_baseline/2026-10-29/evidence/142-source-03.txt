  - heading "1. Know When to Water" [level=2]: 1. Know When to Water
  - paragraph: Watering is one of the most important parts of Encore Azalea care in the summer, but it is not as simple as watering on a set schedule. Heat, rainfall, soil type, mulch depth, and planting location can all affect how quickly the root zone dries out.
  - paragraph: Encore Azaleas prefer soil that stays evenly moist, but not soggy. During hot weather, the surface may look dry while the soil underneath still has moisture. On the other hand, a plant can look stressed if the original root ball has dried out, even when the surrounding soil feels damp. Before watering, check the soil about 2 to 3 inches below the surface near the root ball. If it feels dry at that depth, it is time to water. If it feels damp, cool, or sticky, wait and check again later.
  - paragraph: This is especially important for newly planted Encore Azaleas. During the first growing season, their roots are still spreading into the surrounding soil, which means they may need more frequent attention during hot, dry stretches than established plants.
  - heading "How to Check Soil Moisture" [level=3]
  - paragraph: Both underwatering and overwatering can stress azaleas, so soil moisture is the best place to start. The goal is not to keep the soil constantly wet. The goal is to keep the root zone consistently moist, with enough airflow and drainage to support healthy roots.
  - paragraph: Do not water just because the top of the mulch looks dry. Mulch often dries out faster than the soil underneath, so a quick check below the surface gives you a better idea of what the roots actually need.

    - text: 2. Water Deeply at the Root Zone
  - paragraph:
    - text: Once the soil is dry a few inches down, the next step is

    - text: . A quick sprinkle may wet the leaves, mulch, or top layer of soil without reaching the roots where moisture is needed most.
    - text: Watering deeply helps moisture move into the root zone, where your Encore Azaleas need it most during the summer heat.
  - paragraph: Water slowly at the base of the plant so the soil has time to absorb moisture. If water starts running off, pause for a few minutes and then water again. Dry soil can sometimes repel water at first, so watering in slower rounds can help moisture soak in more evenly. Morning is often the best time to water. It gives the plant moisture before the hottest part of the day and allows any splashed foliage to dry more quickly.
  - heading "Signs Your Azaleas May Need Water" [level=3]
  - list:
    - listitem: Dry soil 2 to 3 inches below the surface
    - listitem: Leaves wilting and not recovering by evening
    - listitem: Curling leaves during hot, dry weather
    - listitem: Brown or crispy leaf tips and edges
    - listitem: Soil pulling away from the root ball or container edge
  - paragraph: Some afternoon wilting can happen during intense heat, even when the soil has moisture. If the plant perks back up in the evening, it may simply be responding to the hottest part of the day. If it stays wilted after temperatures cool, check the soil and water deeply if the root zone is dry.

  - heading "3. Refresh Mulch to Protect the Roots" [level=2]: 3. Refresh Mulch to Protect the Roots
  - paragraph: Mulch is one of the simplest ways to help Encore Azaleas handle the summer heat. A proper mulch layer helps hold moisture in the soil, regulate soil temperature, and protect the root zone from extreme conditions.
  - paragraph: Use pine straw, pine bark, or another organic mulch around the base of your Encore Azaleas. Spread it in a wide, even layer around the plant so it covers the root zone. The key is proper placement. Mulch should protect the roots without sitting directly against the stems.
  - heading "How Much Mulch Should You Use?" [level=3]
  - paragraph: "Keep mulch no more than about 3 inches deep. Spread it outward around the plant instead of mounding it up at the base. Good mulch placement should be:"
  - list:
    - listitem: Wide enough to cover the root zone
    - listitem: Loose enough for water to soak through
    - listitem: Kept a few inches away from the stems
    - listitem: No more than 3 inches deep
  - paragraph: Avoid piling mulch against the trunk or lower stems. Too much mulch around the base can trap moisture, reduce airflow, and create unnecessary stress for the plant.
  - heading "Watch for Reflected Heat" [level=3]
  - paragraph: Where your Encore Azaleas are planted can make a big difference in the summer. Beds near sidewalks, driveways, patios, brick walls, stone edging, or concrete foundations may be hotter than surrounding areas because hard surfaces absorb and reflect heat. Plants in these areas may dry out faster during extreme heat, so the root zone may need closer attention in the summer months.
  - heading "4. Avoid Extra Stress While Plants Are Adjusting" [level=2]: 4. Avoid Extra Stress While Plants Are Adjusting
  - paragraph: When summer temperatures soar, the best care is often simple and steady. If your Encore Azaleas are already showing signs of stress, avoid anything that could make recovery harder. That means being careful with fertilizer, pruning, and unnecessary disturbance around the roots.
  - heading "Be Careful with Fertilizer" [level=3]
  - paragraph: Fertilizer can support healthy growth, but it should not be used as a quick fix for a stressed plant. If an Encore Azalea is wilted, dry, heat-stressed, or struggling, focus first on watering, drainage, and root-zone health.
  - paragraph: Fertilizing a stressed plant can add more pressure when the roots are not ready to take up nutrients efficiently. Wait until the plant is stable and properly hydrated before feeding. If you do fertilize in summer, use a product labeled for azaleas, camellias, or acid-loving plants and follow the package directions carefully. Avoid fertilizing after August.
  - heading "Prune Lightly, If Needed" [level=3]
  - paragraph: Summer is not the time for major shaping or heavy pruning. Encore Azaleas are best pruned right after the spring bloom cycle if shaping is needed. However, dead, damaged, or broken branches can be removed as needed. Removing dead wood can help clean up the plant without taking away healthy growth.
  - heading "How to Tell if a Branch Is Dead" [level=4]
  - paragraph: Use the scratch test. Gently scratch the bark with your fingernail or pruners. If you see green underneath, the branch is still alive. If it is brown, dry, and brittle inside, that section can be removed. When removing dead wood, cut back to healthy green growth or remove the dead branch at its base.

  - heading "What About Container-Grown Encore Azaleas?" [level=3]: What About Container-Grown Encore Azaleas?
  - paragraph:
    - text: In-ground plantings are not the only Encores that need extra attention in the summer months.

      - /url: https://qk5vj4ljbc7ocqxv-75575459876.shopifypreview.com/blogs/plant-care/container-planting-encore-azaleas?_pos=3&amp;_sid=b5fd3b521&amp;_ss=r
    - text: can dry out even faster, especially on patios, porches, and driveways.
    - text: Containers are exposed to heat from all sides, and potting mix can dry quickly in full sun, wind, or near hard surfaces. Check container plants often, especially if they are placed near brick, concrete, or other surfaces that hold heat.
  - paragraph: Water thoroughly until moisture runs from the drainage holes. Then let excess water drain away. Encore Azaleas need moisture, but they should not sit in standing water. If water runs straight through the pot without soaking in, the potting mix may be very dry. Water slowly in rounds so the soil has time to absorb moisture.
  - heading "How to Recognize Heat Stress in Encore Azaleas" [level=2]: How to Recognize Heat Stress in Encore Azaleas
  - paragraph: "Heat stress can show up in several ways, and it can sometimes look similar to watering issues. The best first step is always to check the soil. Common signs of summer stress include:"
  - list:
    - listitem: Wilting during the hottest part of the day
    - listitem: Leaves curling or cupping
    - listitem: Brown, crispy leaf tips or edges
    - listitem: Dry soil near the root zone
    - listitem: Leaf drop after a hot or dry spell
    - listitem: Foliage that looks dull or tired
  - paragraph: Wilting does not always mean the plant needs more water right away. Plants can wilt when the soil is too dry, but they can also wilt when roots are stressed from soil staying too wet. Checking the soil helps you decide what to do next.
  - heading "Quick Troubleshooting Guide" [level=3]: Quick Troubleshooting Guide
  - list:
    - strong: Plant is wilting, and soil is dry
    - text: → Water deeply.
    - strong: Plant is wilting, and soil is wet
    - text: → Hold off on watering and check drainage.
    - strong: Leaves are crispy, and soil is dry
    - text: → The plant may have dried out during heat. Water deeply and monitor.
    - strong: Leaves are yellowing, and soil is constantly wet
    - text: → The root zone may be too saturated. Reduce watering and improve drainage if needed.
    - strong: Plant looks stressed near concrete or brick
    - text: → Reflected heat may be increasing water needs.
  - heading "Keep the Color Coming" [level=2]: Keep the Color Coming
  - paragraph: The summer heat can be tough on the garden, but smart summer care can make a big difference. By supporting the roots, watering based on soil moisture, refreshing mulch, and avoiding unnecessary stress, you can help your Encore Azaleas stay healthy through midsummer and ready for the color still to come.
