    - heading "How To Use Apple Cider Vinegar In The Garden For Weeds, Bugs, And Other Backyard Problems" [level=1]
    - paragraph: It’s more than a cooking ingredient.
    - text: By
    - link "Karen Brewer Grossman":
      - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/karen-brewer-grossman-11756399
    - text: "|"
    - generic: Published on September 21, 2026
    - link "Add Us On":
      - /url: https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=southernliving.com
      - generic: Add Us On
    - 'figure "Credit: Tammi Mild/Getty Images"':
      - img "Rabbit Looking at Garden"
      - text: "Credit:"
      - paragraph: Tammi Mild/Getty Images
    - paragraph:
      - text: Using vinegar to clean around the house is not a new concept. You likely use it for everything from cleaning kitchen surfaces to neutralizing pet odors. And it’s not just white vinegar that’s a workhorse around the house. The same bottle of
      - link "apple cider vinegar":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/apple-cider-vinegar-uses-11976870
      - text: you use for marinades and salad dressings is an effective cleaner as well. Step out into your garden, and there are even more clever uses. Since apple cider vinegar isn’t as acidic as white vinegar, it’s a gentler remedy for gardening problems like adjusting soil acidity. It helps manage pests, clean tools, and keep weeds from taking over your flower beds. Check out these clever uses for apple cider vinegar in the garden and you just may decide to dedicate a bottle to your garden shed permanently.
    - heading "Kill Weeds" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: When it comes to home gardening, natural solutions are an easy answer to avoiding harsh chemicals. Apple cider vinegar is strong enough to control weeds, but it should be used with care. A little spritz on your vegetable plants could burn the leaves or worse. That same effect is good for treating weeds though because the vinegar dries out the part of the plant growing above ground. Keep in mind that vinegar only kills the part of the plant that it touches, so the roots may continue to grow and new weeds can pop up. Vinegar is best for
      - link "weeds in cracks":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/ways-rid-of-driveway-weeds-11982329
      - text: and gravel paths or where no other plants are growing nearby.
    - paragraph: To apply, you can use straight vinegar, diluted vinegar, or make your own weed killer using 4 cups of apple cider vinegar, ½ cup of salt, and a teaspoon of dish soap. Spray carefully to target only weeds.

    - heading "Clean And Sanitize Garden Tools" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: Keep garden tools sanitized between uses by wiping them down with apple cider vinegar. It cuts through grime, mineral deposits, and rust, and it can be used to sanitize tools by spraying them with apple cider vinegar between cuts to prevent spreading diseases from one plant to another. To clean dirt and rust, spray the tools directly with apple cider vinegar, then scrub with a brush and rinse. For stubborn grime or
      - link "heavy rust":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/how-to-clean-rust-off-a-cast-iron-pan-11856490
      - text: ", soak the tools in a mixture of equal parts vinegar and water for four hours to overnight. Scrub the loosened dirt or rust away, rinse, and dry."
    - heading "Prevent And Treat Fungal Diseases" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: Diluted apple cider vinegar can keep
      - link "powdery mildew":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/how-to-protect-plants-from-powdery-mildew-11986686
      - text: ", blight, black spot, and other fungal diseases from developing. The acetic acid it contains gives it natural antimicrobial properties that make it effective as a fungicide, but it should be highly diluted and used with caution to avoid burning foliage."
      - generic: "[ 1 ]"
      - text: Mix 1 to 2 tablespoons of apple cider vinegar with a gallon of water to make a spray to treat affected plants or prevent fungal disease.
    - heading "Manage Pests" [level=2]
    - paragraph: If gnats, fruit flies, mosquitoes, or flies are making spending time outdoors less than peaceful, apple cider vinegar can help. It will also save your plants from hungry snails and slugs.
    - paragraph:
      - text: Trap flies and gnats by
      - link "making your own trap":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/apple-cider-vinegar-fruit-fly-trap-11997680
      - text: to capture them. Mix 2 tablespoons of apple cider vinegar, 1 tablespoon of sugar, and a few squirts of dish soap in a shallow dish to set around your patio. The sugar will lure gnats and the soap will cause them to drown when they check it out. To
      - link "keep flies off your porch":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/how-to-keep-flies-off-your-porch-12000294
      - text: ", omit the sugar. Add a drop of dish soap to a shallow dish and cover it with apple cider vinegar to trap flies."
    - paragraph: To repel mosquitoes, combine ¼ cup each of apple cider vinegar and water, and add 40 or more drops of rosemary or lavender essential oils. Spray the solution on your skin to prevent mosquito bites.
    - paragraph: Apple cider vinegar can even get rid of the slugs and snails that are responsible for eating the delicate leaves from your plants. Spray a mixture of equal parts apple cider vinegar and water directly on snails and slugs to dehydrate and kill them, being careful not to get it on the plants you are trying to save. You can also spray the solution around your garden’s perimeter to keep them from accessing the tender, young plants they love.
    - heading "Keep Deer And Rabbits Away" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: Many animals are turned off by the strong smell of vinegar. Use apple cider vinegar to keep deer, rabbits, and rodents from visiting and feasting on your plants. The best way to
      - link "deter rabbits":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/how-to-keep-rabbits-out-garden-12003550
      - text: and other animals without harming your plants is to soak cotton balls or rags in apple cider vinegar, set them in a dish so they won’t directly contact the soil or plant, and set them around the perimeter of the beds you want to protect. This isn’t a set-it-and-forget-it solution. The smell evaporates and will wash away in rain, so you’ll need repeated applications of this temporary fix. Animals that are desperate for food may eat your plants anyway.
    - heading "Acidify The Soil" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: If your soil is too neutral, use apple cider vinegar to lower the soil pH and
      - link "make it more acidic":
        - /url: https://www.southernliving.com/pine-needle-mulch-soil-11955322
      - text: for plants like blueberries, azaleas, and gardenias to thrive. Too much can kill helpful bacteria or sterilize your soil, so use it sparingly. Also, avoid spraying vinegar directly on plants because it can burn the foliage, stunt growth, or kill beneficial insects. To adjust soil pH, add a tablespoon to a gallon of water, and water the plant at its base to avoid direct contact with foliage. For a long-term fix, amend the soil with organic material like sphagnum peat moss or use acidic fertilizer instead.
