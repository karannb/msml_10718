Keep them out of direct sun outdoors to avoid scorched leaves. 💧 WATER AND TAP WATER WARNING Water only when completely dry. Spider plants are extremely sensitive to fluoride, chlorine, and salts in tap water, which cause brown leaf tips. Use distilled water, rainwater, or reverse osmosis to keep the foliage happy. 👶 BABIES AND BLOOMS To trigger spiderettes, keep the plant slightly root-bound in bright light. Once the offset plantlets form tiny roots at their bases, snip them off and put them directly into damp soil or water to root. 🪴 SOIL AND POTTING Plant in a light, fast-draining mix of 2 parts standard potting soil and 1 part perlite. Spider plants have thick, fleshy tubered roots that store water, so use a pot with drainage holes to prevent root rot. 🌡️ TEMP AND HUMIDITY They thrive in standard home temperatures between 60°F and 80°F. Average indoor humidity works fine, but higher humidity prevents the leaf tips from drying out. 🧪 FEEDING Feed LIGHTLY once a month during spring and summer with an organic balanced houseplant fertilizer diluted to half strength. Avoid over-fertilizing, as excess salts will burn the root tips and cause brown leaf margins. #easycare #gardening #plants #spiderplant #plantsofinstagram"

A plant in the wrong pot can become a constant battle with watering, root rot, drying out, and even temperature stress. Here’s how I choose my pots: 🏺 TERRACOTTA: Best for plants that like to dry out between waterings. It’s VERY porous, so moisture evaporates through the sides and the soil dries faster. Great for succulents, cacti, snake plants, hoyas, and other plants that hate staying wet. The downside? You’ll need to water more frequently so avoid putting anything moisture sensitive into this material!!! 🪴 PLASTIC: Holds onto moisture longer because it doesn’t breathe. This is great for plants that prefer consistently moist soil or for anyone who tends to forget to water. It’s also lightweight, inexpensive, and doesn’t heat up or cool down as quickly (except black plastic pots which conduct heat in direct sun) 🤍 CERAMIC: Glazed ceramic behaves much more like plastic because the glaze prevents water from evaporating through the pot. Unglazed ceramic is more breathable! Ceramic is also heavier, which can be a huge advantage for large plants that get top heavy! 💧 DRAINAGE I don’t care how gorgeous the pot is… if there’s no drainage hole, I don’t want my plant living in it. A decorative pot without drainage can work as a cachepot: keep your plant in a nursery pot with drainage and drop it inside.
