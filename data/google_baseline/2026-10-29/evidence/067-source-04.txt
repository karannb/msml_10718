        - generic: "Your Monstera seems to be experiencing some issues, likely due to overwatering or underwatering. Here are some potential causes and solutions:"
        - generic: "- *Overwatering:*"
        - generic: "- *Symptoms:* Wilting, yellowing leaves, soft stems, and a sour smell from the soil."
        - generic: "- *Solution:* Check the soil moisture by sticking your finger into the soil up to two inches deep. If it's soggy, let it dry out before watering again. Ensure your pot has drainage holes to prevent waterlogging. If root rot has set in, trim away mushy roots and repot in fresh, airy mix."
        - generic: "- *Underwatering:*"
        - generic: "- *Symptoms:* Crispy leaf tips, curling leaves, and a feather-light pot."
        - generic: "- *Solution:* Water thoroughly until water runs out of the drainage holes. Then, wait until the top two inches of soil dry before watering again. Consider bottom watering for 20-30 minutes to ensure the entire root ball is moist."
        - generic: "- *Other potential issues:*"
        - generic: "- *Too much direct sun:* Brown burn marks on leaves. Move the plant to bright, indirect light or use a sheer curtain to filter the sun."
        - generic: "- *Nutrient deficiency:* Yellowing leaves with green veins. Fertilize with a diluted soluble 20-20-20 fertilizer during the spring and summer."
        - generic: "- *Low humidity:* Brown edges or curling leaves. Mist the leaves, add a humidifier, or set the plant on a tray with pebbles and water ¹ ² ³."
        - generic: Given your Monstera has grown three leaves and one has rotted off, it's possible that overwatering is the culprit. Check the soil moisture and adjust your watering schedule accordingly. If the problem persists, consider repotting in fresh mix and pruning any damaged roots ².
