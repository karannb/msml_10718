        - button "Patrick Crosley":
          - generic: Patrick Crosley
        - generic: "There are a number of reasons why a Monstera plant's leaves might turn yellow, including:"
        - generic: Watering
        - generic: Overwatering or underwatering can cause yellow leaves. If the leaves are dry and brittle, the plant might be underwatered. If the leaves are moist and rotting, the plant might be overwatered.
        - generic: Light
        - generic: Monstera plants prefer bright, indirect light. Direct sunlight can burn the leaves.
        - generic: Humidity
        - generic: Low humidity can cause the leaves to brown around the edges and eventually turn yellow. Misting the leaves can help increase humidity.
        - generic: Temperature
        - generic: Temperatures above 90°F can stress the plant and cause leaves to turn yellow.
        - generic: Pests
        - generic: Spider mites and whitefly can drain the plant of moisture, causing the leaves to turn yellow.
        - generic: Natural shedding
        - generic: It's normal for older leaves at the bottom of the plant to turn yellow as the plant sends energy to new growth.
        - generic: Fungal or bacterial disease
        - generic: Yellow spots on the leaves could be caused by a fungal or bacterial infection from an injury to the plant. Pruning the infected leaves can help prevent the disease from spreading.
