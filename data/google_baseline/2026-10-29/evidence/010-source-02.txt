    - paragraph:
      - text: Croton plants (
      - strong: Codiaeum variegatum
      - text: ) are loved for their vibrant leaves, colorful patterns, and tropical appearance. With shades of green, yellow, orange, and red, they can instantly brighten an indoor space.
    - paragraph: However, Crotons are also known for being sensitive plants. A sudden change in environment, improper watering, or insufficient lighting can quickly cause problems such as yellow leaves, leaf drop, and weak growth.
    - paragraph: If your Croton looks stressed or unhealthy, don’t give up. In many cases, the plant is not dying — it is simply showing signs that its growing conditions need adjustment.
    - paragraph: This guide explains the most common reasons Croton plants struggle and how to help them recover.
    - heading "Common Signs Your Croton Plant Is Stressed" [level=3]
    - paragraph: "A struggling Croton may show:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Yellowing leaves
      - listitem: Brown or crispy leaf edges
      - listitem: Drooping leaves
      - listitem: Sudden leaf drop
      - listitem: Pale colors
      - listitem: Slow or weak new growth
    - paragraph: "These symptoms are often caused by a combination of:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Improper watering
      - listitem: Poor soil drainage
      - listitem: Sudden light changes
      - listitem: Insufficient indoor light
    - paragraph: Before making changes, it is important to identify the real cause.
    - 'heading "1. Overwatering: The Most Common Cause of Croton Problems" [level=3]'
    - paragraph: Based on the situation shown above, watering every day is likely one of the biggest problems.
    - paragraph: Although Crotons prefer evenly moist soil, their roots also need oxygen. When soil stays wet for too long, roots can become stressed and begin to rot.
    - paragraph: "Signs of overwatering include:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Yellow leaves
      - listitem: Soft or dropping leaves
      - listitem: Wet soil that stays moist for many days
      - listitem: A musty smell from the pot
    - paragraph:
      - strong: How to Water a Croton Correctly
    - paragraph: Instead of watering on a fixed schedule, check the soil first.
    - paragraph: "Water when:"
    - paragraph:
      - text: ✓ The top 1–2 inches of soil feels dry
      - text: ✓ The pot feels lighter than usual
      - text: ✓ The plant begins to slightly lose firmness
    - paragraph: Avoid watering when the soil is still wet.
    - heading "2. Check the Soil and Roots" [level=3]
    - paragraph: "When you receive a plant from someone else, you may not know:"
    - list:
      - listitem: What type of soil was used
      - listitem: How long it has been in the same pot
      - listitem: Whether the roots are healthy
    - paragraph: "A healthy Croton root system should have:"
    - paragraph:
      - text: ✓ Firm roots
      - text: ✓ White, cream, or light brown color
      - text: ✓ No unpleasant smell
    - paragraph: "Unhealthy roots may appear:"
    - paragraph:
      - text: ✗ Black or mushy
      - text: ✗ Soft and damaged
      - text: ✗ Rotten-smelling
    - paragraph: "If root rot is present:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Remove damaged roots carefully
      - listitem: Replace old soil with fresh potting mix
      - listitem: Use a container with drainage holes
    - heading "3. Choose a Well-Draining Soil Mix" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Crotons need soil that can hold moisture while allowing excess water to drain.
    - paragraph: "A suitable indoor mix can include:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Indoor potting soil
      - listitem: Perlite for drainage
      - listitem: Coco coir for moisture balance
      - listitem: Compost for nutrients
    - paragraph: "The ideal condition is:"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: Moist, but never waterlogged.
    - paragraph: Heavy garden soil or compacted soil can reduce oxygen around the roots and increase the risk of root problems.
    - heading "4. Avoid Sudden Changes in Sunlight" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Crotons enjoy bright light, but sudden changes can stress the plant.
    - paragraph: "For example:"
    - paragraph: A Croton that lived indoors in moderate light should not immediately be placed under several hours of strong direct sunlight.
    - paragraph: "This can cause:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Sunburn
      - listitem: Bleached patches
      - listitem: Brown damaged areas
    - paragraph: Instead, increase light gradually.
    - paragraph: "A better approach:"
    - heading "First stage:" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Place the plant in bright indirect light.
    - heading "After adjustment:" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Slowly introduce more morning sunlight.
    - heading "Long term:" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Maintain consistent bright lighting.
    - heading "5. Why Light Is Important for Croton Recovery" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Light is one of the most important factors affecting Croton health.
    - paragraph: "With insufficient light, Crotons may develop:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Less colorful leaves
      - listitem: Smaller new growth
      - listitem: Longer, weaker stems
      - listitem: Slower recovery
    - paragraph: "Indoor environments can be challenging because:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Windows may not provide enough intensity
      - listitem: Seasonal daylight changes
      - listitem: Plants placed far from windows receive much less light than expected
    - paragraph: A consistent light source can help create a more stable growing environment.

    - heading "6. Should You Remove Damaged Croton Leaves?" [level=3]
    - paragraph: "Only remove leaves that are:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Completely brown
      - listitem: Fully dried
      - listitem: No longer contributing to the plant
    - paragraph: Avoid removing partially damaged leaves too quickly.
    - paragraph: Even stressed leaves can still help the plant produce energy while it recovers.
    - heading "How Long Does It Take for a Croton to Recover?" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Recovery depends on the severity of stress.
    - paragraph: "Generally:"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "After 2–4 weeks:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Leaf drop may slow down
      - listitem: The plant may look more stable
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "After 1–3 months:"
    - list:
      - listitem: New healthy leaves may appear
      - listitem: Growth may become stronger
    - paragraph: "Remember:"
    - paragraph: Damaged leaves usually will not return to their original colors.
    - paragraph: The goal is not to repair old leaves — it is to create better conditions for new growth.
    - 'heading "Final Thoughts: A Stressed Croton Can Recover" [level=3]'
    - paragraph: A Croton with yellow leaves and drooping growth does not always mean the plant is dying.
    - paragraph: "The most common problems are:"
    - paragraph:
      - text: ✓ Overwatering
      - text: ✓ Poor drainage
