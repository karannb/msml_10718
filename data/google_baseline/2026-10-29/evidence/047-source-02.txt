        - generic: Wilting manifests itself if a plant is dry but I’m wondering if the soil is wet & wilting. It does see to me if your plant was dry you’d just water it and it would stand erect again. I’m wondering it its roots have rotted?
        - generic: Your Croton plant (Codiaeum variegatum), showing drooping, limp leaves which can indeed indicate that the root system is suffocating or damaged roots.
        - generic: When a plant wilts despite the soil being wet or recently watered, the roots are failing to function. Overwatering fills the air pockets in the soil, starving the roots of oxygen and encouraging fungal pathogens to rot them away. Since rotten roots can no longer absorb water, the top of the plant dehydrates and wilts, mimicking the appearance of underwatering.
        - generic: "To confirm root rot and potentially save your plant, look for these key symptoms and take immediate action:"
        - img "⚠️"
        - text: How to Confirm Root Rot
        - generic: "* Soil Check: If the soil feels dense, soggy, or remains wet for many days without drying out, it is a primary environment for rot."
        - generic: "* Foul Odor: Sniff the base of the plant. Healthy soil smells earthy, but rotting roots produce a distinct sour, marshy, or sulfur-like smell."
        - generic: "* Visual Inspection: Gently slide the plant out of its pot. Healthy croton roots should be firm and light tan or cream-colored. If they are brown, black, slimy, and easily fall apart when touched, root rot is active."
        - img "🛠️"
        - text: Emergency Steps to Save the Plant
        - generic: "If you check the roots and find they are rotting, follow this extraction and treatment process:"
        - generic: "1. Prune the Dead Roots: Carefully rinse the soil away from the roots with lukewarm water. Use sterilized shears to cut off all mushy, dark roots, leaving only the firm, healthy sections behind."
        - generic: "2. Repot in Fresh Soil: Discard the old, contaminated soil. Clean the container thoroughly with soap and water if you plan to reuse it. Replant the croton in fresh, well-draining soil mix (such as a standard potting mix amended with plenty of perlite or orchid bark to ensure proper aeration). Ensure the pot has functional drainage holes."
        - generic: "3. Trim Top Growth: Prune back some of the top foliage. Since you have reduced the root mass, the remaining roots cannot support a heavy canopy. Cutting back a few leaves helps balance the plant and reduces overall stress."
        - generic: "4. Hold off on Fertilizer: Do not fertilize a recovering plant. The delicate, remaining roots can easily burn from the nutrients while trying to heal."
        - generic: Moving the plant indoors is a good idea to protect it from harsh direct sunlight, wind, and temperature fluctuations while it recovers, but make sure to place it in a spot that still receives bright, indirect light.
