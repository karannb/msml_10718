    - heading "Dried out, dying leaves" [level=2]
    - paragraph: First of all, it's important to keep in mind that dying leaves are a natural part of every plant's life — and succulents are no exception. This doesn't always mean that your succulent is dying, or that you're doing anything wrong.
    - paragraph: If the bottom leaves of your succulent are dried up, it is likely still healthy, but may need to be watered a tiny bit more frequently.
    - paragraph: However, as your plant grows, it creates new leaves, while the older ones die. So, if you're seeing a few dry, crispy leaves at the bottom of the plant–and only at the bottom– there's no need to worry. This is totally normal!
    - paragraph: On the other hand, if most of your succulents leaves are drying up, take that as a sign it wants to be watered a little more often.
    - paragraph: If the dry leaves start to get unsightly, just gently pull them away from the base of the plant and throw them away. When you remove the leaves, keep your plant potted so you don't disturb the roots.
    - generic: ···
    - paragraph: Only pull off the leaves that come off easily, or are totally dead. Here, I pulled the plant out of the pot to better show you what dead leaves on a healthy plant look like.

    - heading "Over-watering" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: While dead leaves at the bottom of your succulent are perfectly healthy, dead leaves on the upper parts of new growth are a sign of a problem–usually over- or under-watering.

    - heading "How to save an over-watered succulent with a black stem" [level=2]
    - paragraph:
      - text: The best way to avoid over-watering is to make sure your soil is completely dried out before watering again. As I've said in a lot of my
      - link "other articles":
        - /url: https://www.succulentsandsunshine.com/blog/
      - text: ", most succulents can easily go three days (and sometimes even a week or more) without water–so"
      - strong: when it doubt, wait before watering.
    - generic: ···
    - paragraph:
      - text: As soon as you notice the symptoms of over-watering on one of your plants, start by cutting back on your watering schedule. You may also need to switch to a better
      - link "soil mixture":
        - /url: https://www.succulentsandsunshine.com/well-draining-soil-for-succulent-container-gardens/
      - text: and be sure you're using a pot with a drainage hole.
    - paragraph:
      - text: If your succulent has a black stem or black spots, you'll need to do a little surgery to save your plant. This is much easier than it sounds! Just cut off the top of your plant, trim away any black spots, give the cutting three to five days to dry out, then
      - link "propagate":
        - /url: https://www.succulentsandsunshine.com/how-to-propagate-succulents-from-leaves-and-cuttings/
      - text: it in new soil.
    - paragraph: On the cuttings below, you can see how I cut off every part of the stem that was soggy or blackened.

If you're lucky, a few days of drying-out time will allow the plant to recover from the over-watering, and it may start to put off new growth.
