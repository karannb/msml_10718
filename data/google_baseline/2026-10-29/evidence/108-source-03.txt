- heading "Common Houseplant Care Mistakes to Avoid" [level=1]
- heading "1. Overwatering or Underwatering Your Plants" [level=2]
- paragraph: Watering can feel like one of the trickiest parts of indoor plant care, but finding the right balance is crucial to avoid overwatering or underwatering.
- list:
  - strong: "Risks of Overwatering:"
  - text: Too much water can lead to root rot, mold, and fungal issues in the soil. If leaves start yellowing or the soil smells funky, you’re likely overwatering.
  - strong: "Risks of Underwatering:"
  - text: On the flip side, underwatering causes dry, wilted leaves, browning edges, and stunted growth.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "How to Fix It:"
- list:
  - listitem: Use a moisture meter to gauge when your plants need water.
  - listitem: Check the top inch of soil with your finger—if it feels dry, it’s time to water.
  - listitem: Always adjust based on the plant’s specific needs. Succulents, for instance, need far less water than ferns.
- img "plant by a windowsill"
- heading "2. Not Providing Enough Light" [level=2]
- paragraph: Light is essential for plants—and every plant has its own preferences. Failing to meet their light needs can result in stretched-out, leggy or elongated growth and yellowing leaves.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "Signs of Light Deficiency:"
- list:
  - listitem: Plants that are leaning toward a window or stretching upwards with thin, weak stems.
  - listitem: Yellowing or dropping leaves, especially in lower-light areas.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "How to Fix It:"
- list:
  - listitem: Research your plant’s light needs. Snake plants thrive in low light, while monsteras prefer bright, indirect light.
  - listitem: Move plants closer to windows or invest in grow lights if natural light is limited.
  - listitem: Rotate plants periodically to ensure even light exposure.

- heading "3. Using the Wrong Soil and Pots" [level=2]
- paragraph: Not all soils or pots are created equal! Using the wrong type of soil or a container without proper drainage can be detrimental to your plant’s health.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "Common Mistakes:"
- list:
  - text: Planting
  - strong:
    - link "succulents":
      - /url: https://phsonline.org/for-gardeners/gardeners-blog/succulent-care-for-beginners
  - text: in regular potting soil instead of a well-draining cactus mix.
  - listitem: Using pots without drainage holes, which can hold excess water and lead to root rot.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "How to Fix It:"
- list:
  - listitem: Always choose a soil mix tailored to your plant’s needs.
  - listitem: Use pots with drainage holes.
- img "potting a houseplant with soil"
- heading "4. Forgetting to Repot When Necessary" [level=2]
- paragraph: Plants need room to grow, and not repotting in time can result in compacted roots and stagnant growth.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "Signs It’s Time to Repot:"
- list:
  - listitem: Roots growing out of the drainage holes.
  - listitem: Soil that dries out almost immediately after watering.
  - listitem: Noticeable lack of new growth.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "How to Fix It:"
- list:
  - listitem: Choose a pot that’s 1–2 inches larger in diameter than the current pot.
  - listitem: Repot during the plant’s active growth period (spring or early summer is ideal).
  - listitem: Handle the roots gently and add fresh soil during the process.
- heading "5. Ignoring Early Signs of Pests" [level=2]
- paragraph: Pests can be a major issue for indoor plants, and the longer you let them go unnoticed, the harder it is to control them. Early detection is key to preventing a full-blown infestation.
- paragraph:
  - strong: "Common Pests and Early Signs to Look For:"
- list:
  - strong: "Aphids:"
  - text: Small, soft-bodied insects that cluster on leaves and stems, often causing curling or yellowing of the leaves.
  - strong: "Spider Mites:"
  - text: Tiny webs that form on the undersides of leaves, accompanied by a speckled appearance on the leaves.
  - strong: "Mealybugs:"
  - text: White, cotton-like clusters that hide in leaf axils and on the stems.
  - strong: Whiteflies
  - text: ": Small, white, winged insects that gather on the undersides of leaves. They can cause yellowing of leaves and a sticky residue due to the honeydew they secrete."
- paragraph:
  - strong: "How to Get Rid of Bugs in Houseplants:"
- list:
  - listitem: Check your plants regularly, especially on the undersides of leaves and around the soil line, for pests.
  - listitem: Isolate infested plants to prevent the spread to others.
  - listitem: Use natural remedies like neem oil, insecticidal soap, or diatomaceous earth to treat infestations.
  - listitem: For fungus gnats, let the soil dry out between waterings, and use sticky traps to catch the adults.
