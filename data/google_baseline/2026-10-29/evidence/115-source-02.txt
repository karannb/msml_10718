  - text: Stuck leaf? Here’s what to do, and NOT do, to get that leaf out safe.
  - text: "DON’T: The first thing is don’t force it. Yep, do nothing. Resist the urge!"
  - text: A moist cotton-bud can be used to very carefully slip between the leaf and sheath or cataphyll BUT there’s a very (very) high risk of damaging that leaf.
  - text: So this one gets a no from me, but I admit to having done this a few times before I learned my lesson after ripping a new leaf (sad face).
  - text: "DON’T: Also often done, but another I wouldn’t do, is to mist or add drops of water where the leaf is stuck. This is meant to help if the sheath has dried out, to help it soften and release the leaf."
  - text: But if dryness is the issue, it’s safer to just water the soil! If droplets get stuck in those tight spots you risk a fungal infection and rot. If you try this method, timing matters. Morning is the best in the hope water fully evaporates before night.
  - text: "DO: Low humidity is the"
  - link "#1":
    - /url: /explore/tags/1/?hl=en
  - text: reason leaves get stuck, especially in Spring. Increasing humidity is often the best solution.
  - text: This time of year plants get cues to grow. But depending on where you are (I’m in New Zealand), there’s more light, but humidity is nowhere near the highs Summer brings.
  - text: Simply running a humidifier (I use the cordless H2O ones), works wonders to get stuck leaves unstuck without risking damage or rot.
  - text: "DO: Another cause is low light. Not enough light can mean plants can’t photosynthesize at the rate needed to produce enough energy to get that leaf moving. Easy fix. More light!"
  - text: "DO: The last cause, also common in Spring, is not feeding. Growing new leaves needs extra fuel. Indoors that fuel comes in the form of fertiliser to provide the essential minerals plants need. If you haven’t fertilised over winter (and yes, you should), then start now to fill up the tank to power growth."
  - text: For this one light is my fix. Mine get extra humidity already and get fed using the ‘weakly weekly’ method all year-round, but this girl’s been in a spot that doesn’t get enough light.
