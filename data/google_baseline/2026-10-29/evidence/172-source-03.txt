- text: Replacing soil and pruning roots is one of the most traumatic things you can do to a plant, particularly if you are inexperienced. When removing the old soil, you also tear away many of the tiny roothairs that do most of the work. That requires the plant to develop new roots to support the rest of the plant. During that period, the rest of the plant will suffer.
- text: For now, you have to manage a delicate balance between water and oxygen (dryness) that the roots require in order to grow. It will require patience as it will take several months or more, at best.
- text: The main cane will need support until the roots have developed enough to support it.
- text: Give it as much warmth and sunlight as possible. Remove leaves as they die.

- text: "- synonym Yucca elephantipes has suffered quite a bit of stress due to the unnecessary root disturbance during the process of pruning and repotting. It would have been best to keep it in it's original container and allow longer intervals between watering, giving the soil time to dry sufficiently. It's not necessary to strip off viable leaves (which may add to the stress of an already stressed plant) but for aesthetics, it's perfectly fine to remove the older, drying, dead leaves. I don't know that I'd recommend pruning an already stressed plant for propagating, it might be best to wait a few months to give the plant time to recover. Pruning will force branching on the main plant. After lopping off a stem, new growth will eventually emerge just below the cut.
