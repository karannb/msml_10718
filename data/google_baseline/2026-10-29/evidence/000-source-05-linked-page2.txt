    - heading "9. Why Are the Leaves Losing Their Holes?" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Those beautiful holes are one of the main reasons people love Monstera adansonii.
    - paragraph: If your plant is producing young leaves with fewer or no fenestrations, don’t immediately assume that something is seriously wrong.
    - paragraph: "Several factors can influence leaf development, particularly:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Plant maturity
      - listitem: Light availability
      - listitem: Growing conditions
      - listitem: Support and climbing behavior
      - listitem: Overall plant health
    - paragraph: Young leaves may naturally look different from mature leaves.
    - paragraph: If the plant is healthy but the newest leaves aren’t as fenestrated as expected, improving light and providing a climbing support may help over time.
    - heading "10. Watch for Yellow Leaves" [level=1]
    - paragraph: A yellow leaf doesn’t automatically mean that your plant is dying.
    - paragraph: One older leaf occasionally turning yellow can simply be part of the plant’s natural aging process.
    - paragraph:
      - text: However,
      - strong: multiple leaves turning yellow at the same time
      - text: deserve investigation.
    - paragraph: "Possible causes include:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Excessive watering
      - listitem: Poor drainage
      - listitem: Insufficient light
      - listitem: Root problems
      - listitem: Environmental stress
      - listitem: Nutrient issues
    - paragraph: Start by checking the soil rather than immediately adding fertilizer.
    - paragraph: If the soil remains wet for a long time, the problem may be related to moisture rather than nutrition.
    - heading "11. Brown or Crispy Leaf Edges" [level=1]
    - paragraph: Brown edges can have several causes.
    - paragraph: "Common possibilities include:"
    - heading "Dry conditions" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Low humidity or inconsistent watering can contribute to crispy edges.
    - heading "Too much direct sunlight" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Strong sunlight can scorch exposed foliage.
    - heading "Salt accumulation" [level=3]
    - paragraph: Mineral buildup from water or fertilizer can sometimes affect sensitive plants.
    - heading "Root stress" [level=3]
    - paragraph: If roots aren’t functioning properly, leaves may not receive enough water even when the soil appears moist.
    - paragraph:
      - text: The important thing is to look at the
      - strong: whole plant and its environment
      - text: rather than treating one symptom in isolation.
    - heading "12. Check Regularly for Pests" [level=1]
    - paragraph: "Monstera adansonii can be affected by common houseplant pests such as:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Spider mites
      - listitem: Thrips
      - listitem: Mealybugs
      - listitem: Scale
      - listitem: Aphids
    - paragraph: Inspect both sides of the leaves, particularly around leaf joints and new growth.
    - paragraph: "Look for:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Tiny insects
      - listitem: Sticky residue
      - listitem: Fine webbing
      - listitem: Speckled leaves
      - listitem: Distorted new growth
      - listitem: Small bumps on stems or leaves
    - paragraph: Catching an infestation early makes management much easier.
    - heading "13. Feed During Active Growth" [level=1]
    - paragraph: A healthy Monstera adansonii can benefit from appropriate fertilization during its active growing period.
    - paragraph: Use a balanced houseplant fertilizer according to the manufacturer’s directions.
    - paragraph: Avoid assuming that more fertilizer means faster growth.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: Overfertilization can damage roots and create additional problems.
    - paragraph: If your plant is growing slowly, first check its light, watering, roots, and temperature before simply increasing fertilizer.
    - heading "14. Keep the Leaves Clean" [level=1]
    - paragraph: Large leaves collect dust surprisingly quickly.
    - paragraph: A layer of dust can make the plant look dull and may interfere with the leaves’ ability to function efficiently.
    - paragraph: Every so often, gently wipe the leaves with a clean, damp cloth.
    - paragraph: Support the leaf underneath while cleaning so you don’t accidentally damage the delicate foliage.
    - paragraph: Avoid using household oils, mayonnaise, or other homemade “leaf-shine” products.
    - paragraph: The goal is simply to keep the foliage clean and healthy.
    - heading "15. Give It Time to Adjust" [level=1]
    - paragraph: If you recently bought a Monstera adansonii, don’t panic if it doesn’t immediately look perfect.
    - paragraph: Moving from a greenhouse or plant shop into a home represents a major environmental change.
    - paragraph: "The plant suddenly experiences different:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Light
      - listitem: Temperature
      - listitem: Humidity
      - listitem: Watering patterns
      - listitem: Air circulation
    - paragraph: Some adjustment is normal.
    - paragraph: Give it stable conditions instead of constantly moving it from one location to another.
    - heading "A Simple Monstera Adansonii Care Routine" [level=1]
    - paragraph: If you’re unsure where to start, keep things simple.
    - heading "☀️ Light" [level=3]:
      - img "☀️"
      - text: Light
    - paragraph: Bright, indirect light.
    - heading "💧 Water" [level=3]:
      - img "💧"
      - text: Water
    - paragraph: Water when the upper portion of the soil has dried rather than following a rigid calendar.
    - heading "🌱 Soil" [level=3]:
      - img "🌱"
      - text: Soil
    - paragraph: Use a loose, airy, well-draining mix.
    - heading "🪴 Pot" [level=3]:
      - img "🪴"
      - text: Pot
    - paragraph: Always use adequate drainage.
    - heading "🌿 Support" [level=3]:
      - img "🌿"
      - text: Support
    - paragraph: Provide a moss pole or trellis if you want it to climb.
    - heading "✂️ Pruning" [level=3]:
      - img "✂️"
      - text: Pruning
    - paragraph: Remove damaged growth and prune strategically to encourage a fuller shape.
    - heading "💦 Humidity" [level=3]:
      - img "💦"
      - text: Humidity
    - paragraph: Moderate to high humidity is beneficial, particularly in very dry homes.
    - heading "🌱 Fertilizer" [level=3]:
      - img "🌱"
      - text: Fertilizer
    - paragraph: Feed appropriately during active growth without overdoing it.
    - heading "The Biggest Mistake to Avoid" [level=1]
    - paragraph: "If there’s one lesson to remember, it’s this:"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: Don’t try to fix every problem by adding more water.
    - paragraph: Yellow leaves, drooping foliage, slow growth, and brown leaves can have many different causes.
    - paragraph: Before watering, check the soil.
    - paragraph: Before fertilizing, check the light.
    - paragraph: Before repotting, inspect the roots and drainage.
    - paragraph: And before assuming the plant is dying, look at the overall pattern of growth.
    - paragraph: Sometimes a small environmental adjustment is all your Monstera needs.
    - heading "Final Thoughts" [level=1]
    - paragraph:
      - text: "A struggling Monstera adansonii doesn’t necessarily need complicated treatment. In most cases, the key is to recreate a few of the conditions it naturally prefers:"
      - strong: bright filtered light, well-draining soil, appropriate moisture, warmth, humidity, and something to climb.
    - paragraph: Give the plant stable conditions, avoid overwatering, monitor for pests, and be patient with new growth.
    - paragraph: "With a little attention, that struggling vine can eventually become a lush, climbing houseplant with beautiful fenestrated leaves—the kind that makes everyone who visits your home ask:"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: “How did you get your Monstera to look like that?”
