    - paragraph: Healthy plants are your best defense. Properly nourished plants stay vigorous and are less susceptible to infestation and disease.
    - list:
      - strong: "Build Healthy Soil:"
      - text: Enrich soil with compost and nutrients to build strong root systems.
      - strong: "Rotate Crops:"
      - text: Avoid planting the same crops (especially vegetables) in the same location year after year.
      - strong: "Keep it Tidy:"
      - text: Insect infestations often start on nearby weeds or dead foliage before migrating to your healthy plants. Keep garden beds clean.
      - strong: "Water & Feed:"
      - text: Feed and water plants regularly (most annuals need weekly fertilizing).
      - listitem: Inspect plants weekly so problems are caught early.

    - heading "Common Plant Pests to Watch For" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Home gardeners typically encounter only a few common pests. Below are the most frequent offenders, how to identify them, and what to do if they appear.
    - heading "1. Aphids" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - img "Close up of green aphids clustering on a plant stem"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Identification:"
      - text: Tiny yellow, green, or dark-colored soft-bodied insects. They tend to cluster on the undersides of leaves, stems, and flower buds.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Damage:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Leaves become curled, distorted, and yellowing.
      - listitem: Flowers may appear malformed.
      - listitem: A shiny, sticky substance (honeydew) coats the leaves, which attracts ants.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Remedy:"
      - text: Aphids reproduce rapidly, so act fast.
    - list:
      - strong: "Water Blast:"
      - text: For minor infestations, spray plants with a strong stream of water to knock them off.
      - strong: "Treatment:"
      - text: Spray with
      - strong: Insecticidal Soap
      - text: or
      - strong: Neem Oil
      - text: . Ensure you coat the undersides of leaves.
      - strong: "Clean Up:"
      - text: Remove plant debris in the fall to prevent eggs from overwintering.
    - heading "2. Mealybugs" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - img "White cottony mealybugs on a plant leaf"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Identification:"
      - text: Oval, white insects up to 1/4 inch long. They cluster in white, cottony masses on stems and leaf nodes. Begonias and Coleus are particularly susceptible.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Damage:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Leaves become deformed, withered, and shiny/sticky from honeydew.
      - listitem: Sooty mold may grow on the sticky residue.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Remedy:"
    - list:
      - strong: "Spot Treat:"
      - text: Dip a cotton swab in rubbing alcohol and touch it to the individual bugs to kill them instantly.
      - strong: "Spray:"
      - text: Use
      - strong: Insecticidal Soap
      - text: or an insecticide labeled for mealybugs. You may need to respray every 7 to 10 days to catch newly hatched eggs.
      - strong: "Wash:"
      - text: Gently hose down plants to wash off the sticky honeydew.
    - heading "3. Scale" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - img "Scale insects looking like brown bumps on a stem"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Identification:"
      - text: These look like tiny brown or tan bumps on leaves and stems. They hide under a shell cover that acts as a shield, making them hard to kill.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Damage:"
    - list:
      - listitem: They suck sap from the plant, causing stunted growth.
      - listitem: If present in large numbers, they can kill the plant.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Remedy:"
    - list:
      - strong: "Manual Removal:"
      - text: Clean off light infestations with a cotton ball soaked in rubbing alcohol or scrape them off with a fingernail.
      - strong: "Pruning:"
      - text: Remove and destroy stems that are heavily crusted with scale.
      - strong: "Oil Sprays:"
      - text: Spray with
      - strong: Dormant Oil
      - text: in early spring or
      - strong: Horticultural Oil
      - text: in summer to suffocate the insects under their shells.
    - heading "4. Thrips" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - img "Damage caused by thrips on flower petals"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Identification:"
      - text: Tiny insects resembling brown or straw-colored wood slivers. They are often found deep at the base of flower petals.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Damage:"
    - list:
      - listitem: Silvery-white streaks appear on leaves.
      - listitem: Flower buds turn brown and die before opening (“bud blast”).
      - listitem: Opened flowers look streaked or distorted.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Remedy:"
      - text: Thrips are troublesome during dry spells and are hard to eliminate completely.
    - list:
      - strong: "Spray:"
      - text: Treat with
      - strong: Insecticidal Soap
      - text: ","
      - strong: Neem Oil
      - text: ", or a pyrethrin-based spray."
      - strong: "Repeat:"
      - text: Spray 2 to 3 times at 10-day intervals to break the life cycle.
      - strong: "Deadhead:"
      - text: Pick off and destroy infested flowers immediately.
    - heading "5. Whiteflies" [level=3]
    - paragraph:
      - img "Whiteflies on the underside of a leaf"
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "Identification:"
      - text: Small, white, moth-like insects. When you shake a plant, they fly up in a cloud. The immature nymphs are flat, oval, and found on the undersides of leaves.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Damage:"
    - list:
      - listitem: They suck plant juices, causing yellowing leaves.
      - listitem: They excrete sticky honeydew, leading to sooty mold.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: "The Remedy:"
      - text: Controlling whiteflies is difficult because the adults fly away when you spray.
    - list:
      - strong: "Target the Nymphs:"
      - text: The immature stage doesn’t move. Spray the undersides of leaves weekly with
      - strong: Insecticidal Soap
      - text: or
      - strong: Horticultural Oil
      - text: .
      - strong: "Traps:"
      - text: Yellow sticky traps can help catch the flying adults.
    - heading "When to Use Insecticides" [level=2]
    - paragraph: Chemical controls should always be a last resort. Whenever possible, choose targeted treatments and follow label instructions carefully. Avoid spraying when pollinators are active.

    - paragraph: Healthy plants, regular monitoring, and early intervention are the most effective ways to manage garden pests. By understanding what you’re seeing and acting quickly, most infestations can be controlled before serious damage occurs.
