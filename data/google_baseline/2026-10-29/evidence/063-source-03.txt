  - text: I have no photo documentation, but my worst Agave injuries were sustained courtesy of A. weberi. The bloodiest one was when I got impaled in my thigh by about 1 inch of the terminal of one of the leaves. Hurt like hell, bled like nuts and I still have a scar from that one, but I would say the incredibly itchy rash, I have had on my arms and belly area from getting Agaves juices soaked through a sweaty long sleeved shirt while doing my first ever stump removal, was worse. That just kept coming back, it would go away for a while and then a few days later just come back. Since then it has been the chainsaw on a stick, a pole saw/pruner and tongs when it comes to dealing the juicy stuff...
  - text: Another interesting one was when I hit my head against one of the tall long arms of a very spiny cereus (likely a somewhat monstrose version as it has too many ribs and too many spines) I had to ask my wife to dig the spines out of my head, some were stuck in my skull... Took some effort to get them out. Another interesting cactus injury came after an encounter with a piece of jumping cholla, but since it is not an Agave injury, I will leave that for another thread...

  - text: I had a few puncture wounds in my arms from doing some cleanup in the garden, nothing out of the ordinary as far as I was concerned, until one of my arms began swelling a bit near the site. I blew it off like I always had in the past thinking it would go away on it's own, and instead it got worse, much worse. So to make a long story short, I had a 5 day stay in an isolation room at the hospital and 2 surgeries to clean up a full blown MRSA infection, I had Vancomycin in my IV, a last resort antibiotic.
  - img ":cry:"
  - text: Needless to say, I blew through my yearly out of pocket max for my healthcare plan by the second day in.
  - text: I'm good now, although I still have some stitches that need to be removed next week.
  - text: Leave the agaves alone! And clean any wounds no matter how small with peroxide or other disinfectant. Life threatening complications from an insignificant injury are real, you are not immune to it. If you get poked, seek medical attention asap if the injury does not improve in 2 days. Keep a thermometer handy, a fever is a good indicator your immune system is in overdrive.
