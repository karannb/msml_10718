- generic: Thrips are tiny sap-sucking pests that leave silvery or light brown dry patches and black speckled frass on Monstera adansonii leaves. The damage often looks like yellowing, brown crispy edges, or “rash-like” spots, and new leaves may unfurl already damaged. Many people don’t see the actual bugs because thrips hide on leaf undersides and in curled new growth, but the black dots and papery texture are telltale signs.

- button "Treating and Eradicating Thrips on Your Plant":
  - generic: Treating and Eradicating Thrips on Your Plant
- button "Collapse section":
  - img "Collapse section"
- generic: Most plant owners recommend isolating the plant first, then physically removing bugs with a strong water spray and cutting off badly damaged leaves. For treatment, insecticidal soap or neem oil works for adults, but you’ll need spinosad like Captain Jack’s Dead Bug Brew plus a systemic granule such as Bonide Systemic Granules to kill eggs and larvae in the soil. Repeat sprays every 5-7 days for at least 3-4 weeks, and treat all nearby plants since thrips spread easily.

  - generic: Recovery and Preventing Future Infestations
- button "Collapse section":
  - img "Collapse section"
- generic: After a heavy infestation, Monstera adansonii often becomes leggy and loses lower leaves, but it can recover with a chop-and-propagate approach or by leaving one healthy leaf to help it regrow. Keep the plant quarantined, use yellow sticky traps to catch adults, and consider beneficial mites for a non-chemical option. New growth should come in undamaged once the pests are gone, but expect it to take several weeks and avoid cutting all leaves at once since the plant needs foliage to photosynthesize.
