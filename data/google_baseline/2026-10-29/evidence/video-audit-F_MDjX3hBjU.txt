  - text: "------------------------------ Healthy plants should never stop growing. That means we have two options: 1. The plant needs to be repotted into a container the next size up, or 2. Split the plant apart to form multiple smaller plants. If I like to keep a plant a certain size so it fits nicely into my decor, I split them apart every so often. Here, I'm splitting a philodendron, which is one of the easiest plants to split! Splitting a plant looks scary but can be super beneficial for the plant, allowing more room for roots to grow and new foliage to sprout. To split a plant:"
  - list:
    - listitem: Remove the plant from the container.
    - listitem: Remove and shake off the dirt exposing the roots.
    - listitem: At the crown, where the roots meet the plant, look for multiple main stems of the plant. Depending on the plant, you may be able to break it apart with your hands or with a knife. Cut into a few natural pieces.
    - listitem: Replant these split pieces into separate containers.
  - text: If you're worried about knowing whether a plant is ready to be split, look to see if the plant is filling up its current container. You may even want to slightly pull it out of its container and see if the roots are being hampered by the sides of the pot. Plants are the gift that keeps on giving, growing and multiplying for us. This is a great way to add more to your collection... or have plants to give away!
