I went on vacation for a month with my mom and sister while my brother and dad stayed at home. While we were gone they barely watered my mother’s plant, which has a couple wilted leaves but is otherwise fine. My prayer red plant, however, is another story. While we were gone they forgot I had a plant and didn’t water it a single time. All the leaves were dead and the stem is a sickly yellow brown colour. I removed the dead leaves and unpotted it to see what I’m working with but most of the roots snapped off and are completely brown. The only part intact is a small portion of the roots, stem, and what I think are seeds of some sort. This was the first plant I started taking care of and I’ve had it for 6 years, so I don’t know much about gardening outside of this plant, but I care for it a lot. I wish I could attach an image of what I have left, but I don’t know how to do that. Do you guys think I can still save it, or is it a lost cause?
Answer · 10 votes
All the leaves were dead and the stem is a sickly yellow brown colour. I removed the dead leaves and unpotted it to see what I’m working with but most of the roots snapped off and are completely brown. The only part intact is a small portion of the roots, stem, and what I think are seeds of some sort. Soak it for an hour in some room temperature water, the entire thing immersed. Then repot it in any kind of regular well-draining houseplant soil (not cactus soil). Put it in bright light but no direct hot sun. Water it normally, as the soil is dried out about 1/2" down. Don't dig it up, don't repot it. Just let it alone. If you don't see new growth in a month, it's dead and not coming back.

Question
How do I help my plant which accidentally was watered with salty water and is now dying?
Answer · 9 votes
It depends on how long ago this happened. If you had realized immediately after watering the plant with salty water you you could have removed the plant from the pot washed all of the soil from the roots with clear water and repotted it while hoping for the best. The only other option would have been to try to rinse the soil which would have depleted it of a lot of its nutrients.If your plant has already absorbed some of the water with the salt it may be too late. You can still try removing it from the contaminated soil rinsing the roots and giving it fresh soil. Another thing which might help it would be to cut it back fairly severely to get rid of the contaminated foliage and hoping it grows new healthy foliage. Good luck!

Question
We've recently rescued a bunch of potted plants, one of them we don't know so are not sure about the best way to care for it. It was in a darkened room and didn't get watered for days, possibly weeks. As first care we gave it water (submerging the pot in a bucket to rehydrate the dried-out soil) and put it into the sun - but without knowing its name we can't research what temperatures, sun exposure etc. it needs. Please help us figure out what plant it is, and maybe how to best care for it.
Answer · 8 votes
This is an umbrella plant, likely a Schefflera arboricola or Schefflera actinophylla. It's a common houseplant, often famed for being not too fussy and rather tolerant of periodic neglect. So your chances or reviving it are quite good. You'll find plenty of care instructions (random example), one thing is important for you right now: Scheffleras don't like full sun, so please put it in dappled shade outside (if your temperatures are consistently above 60°F / 16°C) or indoors in a bright spot. This is particularly important because, as Ecnerwal already pointed out, sunburn is a real risk for plants that have been in dark places for a while and not yet acclimated.
