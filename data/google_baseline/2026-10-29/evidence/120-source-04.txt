  - paragraph: You may want to try to find a way to add humidity around the tree. Mites seem to explode in warm dry conditions, so if you can do the pebble tray with water thing (if the trees are small) and/or mist around the trees frequently, that might help. As another suggestion, I have seen posts where people with indoor and/or overwintering citrus, will spray the trees down every once in awhile in the shower stall or in the tub with a shower fixture (this will depend on how large the tree is). I know 2 winters ago, I was able to concoct a "shower station" for mine by sitting them in a big muck bucket, surrounding them with some plastic sheeting tucked into the muck bucket and held up with some tall plastic garden stakes. I then used a hand-held sprayer attached to my basement stationary tub faucet, to spray the leaves/stems/trunks with warmish water, with the muck bucket being the catch basin. I now have a 3 gallon wheeled tank sprayer that I use just for watering (it has a couple spray attachments), so I could probably do the same with that and not need a faucet for the water.

  - paragraph: Miscel,
  - paragraph: Mites can and do develop resistance towards acaricides that act biochemically on their organism. They can't and don't develop resistance to pesticides which act mechanically (e.g. they suffocate them, dissolve their external membrane cells and/or kill their eggs.). Oily products belong to the second category. HO does exhibit some ovicidal action also. The oils in some so called 'Neem oil' products may be able to act similarly but Neem extract products which rely on the plant toxin azirachtin for their action don't.
  - paragraph: Mites are not insects. The use of insecticides against mites is a no-no as most of them exhibit low efficacy towards mites and will kill their insect predators. Acaricides should be used instead if necessary.
