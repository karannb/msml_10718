    - paragraph: Succulent plants get leggy from time to time. Keep reading to learn why it happens and what you can do about succulents with long stems.
    - paragraph: Oh, gorgeous succulents, we do love you, but why do your stems grow long? My garden in Santa Barbara was chock-full of them, but it didn’t bother me when this happened because I had so many. They intertwined, co-mingled, and propped each other up. Every once in a while, I’d cut some back to propagate and/or give away.
    - paragraph: I now live in Tucson, which isn’t the optimal climate for growing fleshy succulents. Mine grow in pots; some can look sad when that intense summer heat rolls in. They all grow in the shade because they can’t handle the hot sun here. One of my succulent plantings (seen below) was due for a total cutback because the stems had gotten long, leggy, and stretched out.

      - strong: 2) They’re reaching for the light source.
    - paragraph: If you’ve positioned them against a wall or in a corner, they’ll start reaching toward the light. This produces longer stems and less new growth.
    - paragraph: This can also happen in the winter months when the sunlight is less plentiful.
    - paragraph:
      - strong: 3) The light they’re growing in is too low.
    - paragraph: This may be true for your leggy succulents, especially if growing indoors. Your succulents may not get enough light, and you may notice leggy stems, paler foliage, and smaller leaves.
    - paragraph: This could be due to the lack of sunlight or artificial light. You’ll have to move your succulents to a place with more sun exposure, get a grow light or two, or, if possible, move indoor succulents outdoors.

    - heading "What Should You Do with the Cuttings?" [level=2]:
      - strong: What Should You Do with the Cuttings?
    - paragraph: Here’s what you do, whether your succulents grow in the ground or a pot, with those tall, stretched-out succulent stems.
    - paragraph: You can propagate by stem cuttings or by leaf cuttings. Stem cuttings are a much faster method, but if you have the patience, try leaf propagation.
    - paragraph: I got quite a few cuttings, as you can see. I put them in a long, low box, then moved them to my utility room with bright light but no direct sun. They need to be in a well-lit area, but they’ll burn if they get too much sunlight. The amount of light they need while healing over isn’t as great as they would need when growing as plants.
    - paragraph: You’ll want to prep the cuttings. Cut off any curved stems and strip off leaves if needed. You want the stems to be as straight as possible because they’re easier to plant.
    - paragraph:
      - text: The cut end of long-stemmed succulents must heal for at least three to six days. Think of this like you would a wound healing over. Cuttings planted back in the soil too quickly before they have a chance to heal are more subject to
      - link "root rot":

      - text: I’ve let some succulents heal over for nine months, and they grew just fine. Succulents with fine stems, like
      - link "String Of Pearls":

      - text: ", only need a couple of days. It’s hot here in Tucson, so I don’t heal succulents over for too long."
    - paragraph: After planting, they’ll have new roots in 1-2 months.

    - heading "1) Remove the top layer of soil (if planting them back in the same pot)." [level=3]:
      - strong: 1) Remove the top layer of soil (if planting them back in the same pot).
    - paragraph: This planting was done two years ago, so the soil mix wasn’t too old or compacted. I removed the top 10″ to make room for a fresh mix. Succulents don’t root too deep, so removing it all wasn’t unnecessary.
    - heading "2) Use a mix formulated for succulents & cacti." [level=3]:
      - strong: 2) Use a mix formulated for succulents & cacti.
    - paragraph:
      - text: Fill or top off the pot with succulent and cactus mix. Succulents need a loose mix that is well aerated so the water can thoroughly drain out and they don’t rot. I use this
      - link "DIY succulent and cactus mix":

      - text: for all my succulent planting, indoors and outdoors.
    - heading "3) Mix in compost." [level=3]:
      - strong: 3) Mix in compost.
    - paragraph: (This is optional, but it’s something I always do). A few handfuls of compost enriches the soil naturally, so the roots are healthy and the plants grow stronger. I mixed some of the fresh blend in with the older soil mix. After the cuttings have rooted in, I’ll apply a 1/2″ to 1″ topping of worm compost. This is my favorite amendment, which I use sparingly because it’s rich.

    - heading "How to Maintain the New Succulent Planting" [level=2]:
      - strong: How to Maintain the New Succulent Planting
    - paragraph: Succulents are some of the easiest plants.
    - paragraph: I let the new planting settle in for about five to seven days before watering the mix. I learned this early on, and it’s always worked well for me.
    - paragraph: I’ll water this planting once a week until the weather cools. You don’t want to keep your cuttings as dry as you would an established plant. Remember, the roots are still forming.
    - paragraph: Conversely, don’t give too much water, or the cuttings will rot out. Adjust according to your conditions.
    - paragraph: Keep your cuttings out of any direct hot sun to avoid burn. Bright natural indirect sunlight (moderate to high light exposure) is the sweet spot.
