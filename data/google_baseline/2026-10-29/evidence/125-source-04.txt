- heading "What's the difference between traditional azaleas and reblooming azaleas?" [level=2]
- paragraph: The main difference between traditional azaleas and reblooming azaleas is the bloom time.
- paragraph: Traditional azaleas bloom in the spring and last for a few weeks. They set their buds on the previous year’s growth, which means they should be pruned after the spring bloom.
- paragraph: Reblooming azaleas bloom in spring and again in late summer/fall, offering months of blooms rather than just weeks. A light trim after the spring bloom will encourage new growth and a more abundant rebloom.

- heading "Why Plant a Reblooming Azalea?" [level=3]
- heading "Floral Display" [level=3]
- paragraph: Azaleas of all kinds are known for their stunning floral displays. The tubular blooms densely cover the compact shrubs, creating an eye-catching show in the garden.
- heading "Long Bloom Time" [level=3]
- paragraph: Why enjoy those floral displays for just one season when you can enjoy them for multiple seasons? Reblooming azaleas offer a beautiful spring bloom, followed by a second bloom in late summer/fall.
- heading "Low Maintenance" [level=3]
- paragraph: Reblooming azaleas are not only beautiful, they are also easy to care for. Thanks to their more compact habits, reblooming azaleas don’t need to be pruned unless desired. A light trim can be done to encourage a more abundant rebloom.
