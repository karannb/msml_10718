You grade an answer to a Reddit post asking what is wrong with a houseplant. The thread is resolved: the poster
followed some advice and said it worked. Compare the answer with that reference.

Reference, from the thread:
- Confirmed problem: {problem}
- Advice given by commenters (the poster followed some or all of it): {answer}
- What the poster said afterwards: {outcome}

Answer to grade:
<answer>
{response}
</answer>

Grade:
- diagnosis: "correct" if the answer's main diagnosis is the confirmed problem; "partial" if the answer names it but
  only as one possibility among several or not as the main cause, or gets only part of it; "wrong" otherwise.
  If the confirmed problem is that the plant is healthy or normal, "correct" means the answer says so.
- false_alarm: true if the confirmed problem is that the plant is healthy or normal and the answer says it has a
  problem that needs treatment; false otherwise.
- advice: "yes" if following the answer's main advice would most likely have produced the same fix; "partial" if the
  fix is in the answer but buried among other suggestions or incomplete; "no" otherwise.
- harmful: true if the answer recommends something, or reassures the owner in a way, that is likely to make this plant
  worse or delay the fix it needs (e.g. telling the owner not to worry about a pest, or combining incompatible
  treatments). Advice protected by a safeguard, such as checking the soil before watering, is not harmful.
- actions: every action the answer asks the owner to take. One item per distinct task, including checks, conditional
  steps ("if the roots are mushy, trim them") and alternatives; skip things to avoid and things to simply keep doing.
  A repeated task is one item, with per_day = how many times it is done on the busiest day (1 for one-off or weekly
  tasks). minutes: realistic time for one occurrence. cost_usd: money for things beyond basic household supplies
  (water, scissors, dish soap, rubbing alcohol, cotton swabs, plastic bags, paper towels); 0 if none. unclear: true if
  the answer doesn't say enough to estimate the time or cost (e.g. "use an appropriate treatment").
- reason: one short sentence explaining the diagnosis, advice and harm grades.
